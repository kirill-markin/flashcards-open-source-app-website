> Ik droog een bord af met **de theedoek**.

*Ik droog een bord af* means “I dry a plate.” Picture what you'd reach for. **De theedoek** is the cloth you use to dry it, a dish towel or tea towel. The action *droog … af* gives you the clue. [Dutch Language Institute entry for *theedoek*](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/theedoek)

The 12 clues below give you that same kind of practice with everyday Dutch vocabulary for household objects and things you use on the go. They’re for English-speaking beginners who can read a short Dutch sentence with some help. Say or write the English name of the object in bold. “Dish towel” is enough. An English description like “the cloth for drying dishes” counts too.

## Read the Dutch nouns, then use the clues

Look at the bold phrase first and see if you know the object. If you need help, read the rest of the sentence and look for an action or a detail that narrows it down. Put a dot next to any answer you got from the sentence so you can try that word on its own later.

The bold phrases include **de** or **het**, the two ways to say “the” with a singular Dutch noun. Read them together as one phrase, as in **de theedoek**. The English answer can just be the name of the object. [Taalunie guide to Dutch articles](https://taaladvies.net/termen-lidwoord/)

1. Na het ontbijt veeg ik de kruimels in **het stofblik**.
2. Het regent. Ik trek **de regenjas** aan voordat ik naar buiten ga.
3. Er ligt zand op het tapijt. Ik haal **de stofzuiger** uit de kast.
4. Met **de garde** klop ik het ei los in een kom.
5. Met **het meetlint** kijk ik hoe breed de tafel is.
6. Mijn fietsband is zacht. Ik haal **de fietspomp** uit de schuur.
7. Met **de wasknijper** maak ik het shirt vast aan de waslijn.
8. Ik hang de natte sokken op **het droogrek**.
9. Met **de deegroller** maak ik het deeg plat voor de koekjes.
10. Ik schijn met **de zaklamp** onder de kast en zie mijn sleutel.
11. Met **de flesopener** haal ik de metalen dop van de limonadefles.
12. De kurk zit nog in de fles. Pak jij **de kurkentrekker** even?

A few words can help you get started. *Kruimels* are crumbs, *tapijt* is carpet, and *plat* means “flat.” *Klop … los* means “beat” in the egg sentence. *Waslijn* means “clothesline,” *schijn* means “shine,” *dop* is a cap, and *kurk* is a cork.

## Check which clue points to each object

1. **Het stofblik** means **dustpan**. *Veeg … in* tells you where the crumbs go. The dustpan catches them as you sweep.
2. **De regenjas** means **raincoat**. *Het regent* means “it's raining.” The speaker puts on the coat before going outside. [Dutch Word List entry for *regenjas*](https://woordenlijst.org/zoeken/?q=regenjas)
3. **De stofzuiger** means **vacuum cleaner**. *Zand op het tapijt*, “sand on the carpet,” points to the machine the speaker takes out of the cupboard. [Dutch Word List entry for *stofzuiger*](https://woordenlijst.org/zoeken/?q=stofzuiger)
4. **De garde** means **whisk**. The speaker uses it to beat an egg in a bowl. [Dutch Word List entry for *garde*](https://woordenlijst.org/zoeken/?q=garde)
5. **Het meetlint** means **tape measure**. *Hoe breed* means “how wide.” The speaker uses the tape to check the table's width.
6. **De fietspomp** means **bike pump**. A soft tire tells you why the speaker fetches the pump from the shed.
7. **De wasknijper** means **clothespin** or **clothes peg**. *Maak … vast* means “fasten.” The clip holds the shirt on the clothesline.
8. **Het droogrek** means **clothes drying rack**. *Natte sokken* are wet socks. You hang them on the rack to dry.
9. **De deegroller** means **rolling pin**. *Ik maak het deeg plat* means “I flatten the dough.” The speaker rolls it flat to make cookies. [Dutch Word List entry for *deegroller*](https://woordenlijst.org/zoeken/?q=deegroller)
10. **De zaklamp** means **flashlight** or **torch**. The speaker shines it under the cabinet and spots the key.
11. **De flesopener** means **bottle opener** here. *Metalen dop*, “metal cap,” points to the tool that lifts the cap off the bottle.
12. **De kurkentrekker** means **corkscrew**. The stopper is a *kurk*, a cork. This tool pulls it out.

Both **het stofblik** and **de stofzuiger** begin with *stof*, meaning “dust.” Look at the second part to tell them apart. A *blik* can be a tin or container, like the pan that catches what you sweep up. In *stofzuiger*, *zuiger* comes from *zuigen*, meaning “to suck.” Dutch writes each compound as one word. [Official Dutch spelling guide to compound words](https://woordenlijst.org/zoeken/leidraad/6/2.html)

## Try a few words without their sentences

Cover the answers and say what **de wasknijper**, **het stofblik**, and **de zaklamp** mean. Check them against answers 7, 1, and 10. Now try the words you marked with a dot. If you need the whole sentence again, use it, then come back to the noun after a few other words. For a second look at the two openers, try **de kurkentrekker** and **de flesopener** side by side and say what each one removes.

Try a few of the same nouns tomorrow in a different order, with the answers covered. Attempting the meaning before checking gives you a chance to retrieve it. Coming back after a gap lets you practice that recall in a later session. Both methods can help you remember the words longer.
