خانه means “house” or “home.” Remembering *khaane* doesn’t necessarily mean you can read خانه, or write it when someone asks for “home.” Good **Persian vocabulary flashcards** let you practise those tasks separately: retrieve a meaning from Persian script, then retrieve selected Persian spellings from English cues.

This guide is for English-speaking beginners learning modern Iranian Persian, also called Farsi. The small practice set works on paper and includes every answer. You’ll practise everyday words, a thank-you response and the difference between a standard written form and a common colloquial form. Use it alongside alphabet and listening practice.

## Keep the Persian visible from the start

Persian is written from right to left. Short vowel marks are usually omitted in ordinary writing, so the letters alone don’t spell out every vowel you need to say. The University of Texas’s [writing-system introduction](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/the-writing-system/) explains both features.

A pronunciation aid helps when you first meet a word. On a recall question, keep it on the answer side. If every question shows *ketaab* beside کتاب, you may answer “book” without reading کتاب at all.

Use these five items for the exercises on this page:

| Persian | Approximate pronunciation aid | Meaning used here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| آب | aab | water |
| کتاب | ketaab | book |
| چای | chaay | tea |
| خانه | khaane | house; home — standard written form |
| ممنونم | mamnunam | thank you — a short response |

The aids approximate Iranian pronunciation. Here, **aa** is roughly the vowel in “father,” pronounced toward the back of the mouth; other systems often write it *ā* or *â*. **a** is roughly the vowel in “cat,” **e** that in “bed,” and **u** “oo” as in “food.” **ch** is like the beginning of “chair,” **y** like the beginning of “yes,” and **kh** like the sound in Scottish “loch.” English accents vary, so these comparisons can only get you started.

When studying Persian words with pronunciation aids, remember what the English letters leave out. This system doesn’t mark stress or fine sound differences, and it isn’t IPA or reversible transliteration: you can’t reliably work backward from it to every Persian spelling. UT’s [system of transcription](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/system-of-transcription/) explains its own convention and recommends training your eye on the Persian characters. Use recordings and feedback for the sounds themselves; the companion deck has no audio.

For individual meaning and pronunciation checks, the Persian sections of the dictionary entries for [آب](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/آب#Persian), [کتاب](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/کتاب#Persian), [چای](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/چای#Persian), [خانه](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/خانه#Persian), and [ممنونم](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ممنونم#Persian) provide further reference. Select the Iranian reading where the entry distinguishes varieties.

## Make recognition and production separate questions

With Farsi flashcards for beginners, start by retrieving the meaning of a familiar word in script. Study the table, then cover its English and pronunciation columns. Look at **کتاب**, answer “book,” and only then uncover the meaning. Recognizing that you’ve seen the word before isn’t the same as recalling what it means.

Later, turn the task around. Cover the Persian column and write the word for “book.” Check the actual letters against **کتاب**. An answer in English letters shows that you recalled a pronunciation aid; it doesn’t show that you recalled the Persian spelling.

Here is a worked pair you can put on paper or into separate cards:

| Task | Front: the question | Back: the answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize script | What does آب mean? | Water. Approximate aid: *aab*. |
| Retrieve script | Write the Persian word for “water” from this practice set. | آب — water. Approximate aid: *aab*. |

Separate the two directions with unrelated questions, or practise them in different rounds. An immediate reverse question may only test whether the previous answer is still fresh. Choose a few words you want to produce; you don’t need to reverse every card automatically.

You can add a situation without demanding a whole sentence. “You want to name the drink tea. Write the word from this set” asks for **چای**. “Someone has helped you. Give the thank-you response introduced here” asks for **ممنونم**. The second cue targets a useful response while allowing that Persian has other ways to thank someone.

## Label خانه and خونه as different forms

The standard written form **خانه** (*khaane*) and the colloquial form **خونه** (*khune*) both refer to a house or home. The latter is common in colloquial Iranian Persian, particularly Tehran speech. Both the Persian spelling shown here and the pronunciation change. Keeping the labels beside the forms helps you match what you read with what you hear.

UT’s [introduction to written and colloquial Persian](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/language-specific-grammar/diglossia/) gives this pair. Its [diglossia rules](https://sites.la.utexas.edu/persian_online_resources/language-specific-grammar/diglossia-readings/) also caution that spoken changes vary in how consistently speakers use them. Learn the specific pair; don’t turn it into an instruction to replace every **aa** with **u**.

A prompt should say which form it wants:

- **Write the standard written word for “home” introduced here.** → خانه
- **What does خونه mean, and which form is it?** → House/home; colloquial, with the approximate aid *khune*.

If you answer خونه to the first prompt, you remembered a relevant word but missed the requested form. Correct the register label. If a prompt merely asks for “home in Persian,” either form may be reasonable in its own context; improve that question instead of treating the ambiguity as your mistake.

## Remove the aid one step at a time

Study the script, meaning and pronunciation aid together for the first encounter. For the next attempt, hide the aid and retrieve the meaning from the script. Check the answer afterward. Once you can do that, try English-to-script questions for a few chosen items, with no Latin-letter hint on the front. You can still consult the aid after answering; withdrawing the hint doesn’t mean discarding pronunciation support.

Check one attempt at a time. If you know what کتاب means but can’t write it from “book,” keep those two results separate. If you remember the meaning but not the vowels, revisit pronunciation with a reliable recording. Don’t count silent word recognition as a pronunciation test.

Return to the material in later sessions, including items you answered correctly. Regular recall and spaced review can support longer-term retention. [Karpicke and Roediger’s vocabulary experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/) found that continued retrieval improved delayed recall. [Cepeda and colleagues’ spacing experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/) found that useful gaps between study sessions depended on how much later the final test occurred. Neither establishes a universal timetable for Persian learners or a promise of permanent memory.

For broader help choosing focused prompts and keeping a vocabulary deck manageable, see [how to use flashcards for language learning](/blog/how-to-use-flashcards-for-language-learning/).

## Try a short round without the table

Read the six questions below, then look away from the notes and answer on a separate sheet. Use the words introduced on this page. For writing questions, produce Persian script without a Latin-letter hint. Check the answers after the round.

1. What does **چای** mean?
2. Write the Persian word for **water**.
3. Someone has done you a small favor. Write the **thank-you response from this set**.
4. What does **کتاب** mean?
5. Write the **standard written form** for **house/home** introduced here.
6. What does **خونه** mean, and is it the standard written or colloquial form in this pair?

### Answers and what to check

1. **Tea** — چای, approximate aid *chaay*. The answer is the drink.
2. **آب** — water, *aab*. Compare the whole spelling, including the mark above the initial letter.
3. **ممنونم** — thank you, *mamnunam*. Other thank-you expressions exist; this question asks for the one introduced here.
4. **Book** — کتاب, *ketaab*. The initial short vowel isn’t explicitly marked in the ordinary spelling shown.
5. **خانه** — house/home, *khaane*. This prompt asks for the standard written form.
6. **House/home; colloquial** — خونه, *khune*. The standard written counterpart in this pair is خانه.

Use the result to choose your next small task: reread a spelling, retrieve a meaning, or compare the two register labels. These six answers don’t measure your overall Persian level. Reading unfamiliar text, understanding speech and joining a conversation require practice beyond this written set.
