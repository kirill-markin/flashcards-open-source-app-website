A flashcard asks for the mean and median of five numbers. You get the median right and miss the mean. How do you grade that card? Before you hand an AI flashcard generator a whole chapter, try it with one short passage. See which facts make it into the deck, how much you have to fix, and how you’ll pick up your next review.

Nibomo publishes this guide and makes one of the tools below. AI assisted with research and writing. The comparison draws on each maker’s published documentation and uses an original set of notes and reference cards. The three tools were not tested hands-on for this guide.

## Give each tool the same notes

Paste this passage as written. If the tool accepts separate instructions, ask for one question per card and tell it to use only the passage. Keep the first draft handy so you can see how many cards you had to change.

> **Class notes on mean and median**
>
> Our sample values are 2, 4, 6, 8, and 30. Find the arithmetic mean by dividing the sum by the number of values. Here the sum is 50, so the mean is 50 ÷ 5 = 10. For an odd number of values, sort them and take the middle value as the median. Here that value is 6. If we replace 30 with 50, the sum becomes 70 and the mean becomes 14. The median stays 6 because the middle value in the sorted list stays the same.

### Six reference cards

Different wording is fine. Check whether the draft lets you recall each idea on its own.

| Front | Back |
| --- | --- |
| How do you calculate the arithmetic mean of a list of numbers? | Divide the sum by the number of values. |
| How do you find the median of a list with an odd number of values? | Sort the values and take the middle one. |
| What’s the mean of 2, 4, 6, 8, and 30? | 10 |
| What’s the median of 2, 4, 6, 8, and 30? | 6 |
| In the list 2, 4, 6, 8, and 30, what’s the new mean if you replace 30 with 50? | 14 |
| Why does the median stay at 6 when the list changes from 2, 4, 6, 8, 30 to 2, 4, 6, 8, 50? | The middle value in the sorted list is still 6. |

### Check the draft, then try a review

1. **Check the facts and coverage.** Correct a card that gives 12 as the new mean. Restore the odd-number condition if the median rule drops it. Leave even-length lists out of this exercise, since the notes only cover odd-length lists. Use the six reference cards to spot useful ideas the draft missed.
2. **Try answering each front by itself.** Split “What are the mean and median?” into two cards that each name the list. Rewrite “Why did it stay the same?” so it names the median and the change to the list.
3. **Save an edit and check it where you’ll study.** Fix a front or back, remove any duplicate, then reopen the deck. If you plan to print or export it, check the revised card in the printed copy or imported deck.
4. **Review once and come back tomorrow.** Say or write your answer before flipping the card. Rate your recall if the tool offers ratings. On the next visit, find where the tool shows what’s due. If you’re using paper or a mode without scheduling, mark the cards you missed and pick a time to retry them.

Once you have a tool that fits your review routine, try it on one page of your own notes. Count how many cards you could keep as written and how many you had to rewrite or add. If fixing the draft takes longer than writing the cards you need, try a shorter passage or make that set by hand. The [AI flashcard cleanup guide](/blog/how-to-fix-ai-flashcards/) has more examples if you’re ready to work through a larger draft.
