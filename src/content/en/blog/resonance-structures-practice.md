## Keep the atoms fixed

Resonance contributors describe one species with different electron placements. Keep the same atoms connected to the same neighbors, including every hydrogen. You can rotate or rearrange a drawing on the page; you can't move a hydrogen from one atom to another. The hybrid doesn't switch between contributors, and the double-headed resonance arrow **↔** doesn't describe an equilibrium. These conventions follow [OpenStax's resonance rules](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/2-5-rules-for-resonance-forms).

Here, **–** means a single bond, **=** a double bond, and **LP** a lone pair containing two electrons. Oa and Ob identify individual oxygen atoms; keep those identities throughout. Lone-pair counts are explicit below.

The exercises cover paired-electron structures containing H, C, N, and O. Hydrogen gets two surrounding electrons; second-period atoms cannot exceed eight. An incomplete octet needs attention, but isn't automatically disqualifying: a positively charged carbon with six electrons can appear in a minor contributor. Audit validity before considering importance.

## A ledger that catches local mistakes

For each atom, record **B**, the sum of its bond orders, and **L**, its number of lone pairs:

- Surrounding electrons: **E = 2B + 2L**.
- Formal charge: **FC = V − B − 2L**, where V is 1 for H, 4 for C, 5 for N, and 6 for O.
- Whole-structure electron count: **2 × (sum of bond orders across unique bonds + sum of all lone pairs)**.

Count each bond once; summing atom E values would count bonding electrons twice. The formal-charge formula and charge-sum check follow [OpenStax's electron bookkeeping](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/7-4-formal-charges-and-resonance). For a slower calculation refresher, use the [formal-charge worked examples](/blog/formal-charge-vs-oxidation-number/).

The two global checks are related: summed formal charge equals the neutral atoms' total valence electrons minus the electrons drawn. Passing both doesn't provide two independent reasons to trust a structure. The local E column catches the overfilled atom that those totals miss.

Copy this blank ledger onto paper:

| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |
| ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ | ____ |

## Work through formate once

Formate, HCO₂⁻, has **1 + 4 + 2(6) + 1 = 18** valence electrons. Begin with **H–C(=Oa)–Ob⁻**: Oa has 2 LP, Ob has 3 LP, and H and C have 0 LP.

| Atom | V | B | L | E | FC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H | 1 | 1 | 0 | 2 | 0 |
| C | 4 | 4 | 0 | 8 | 0 |
| Oa | 6 | 2 | 2 | 8 | 0 |
| Ob | 6 | 1 | 3 | 8 | −1 |

Four bond-order units contain 8 electrons; five lone pairs contain 10. Total: 18. Formal charges sum to −1.

To obtain the other equivalent contributor, use one Ob lone pair to make C=Ob **and** place the C=Oa π pair onto Oa. The result is **H–C(–Oa⁻)=Ob**, with LP counts H 0, C 0, Oa 3, Ob 2. Its ledger swaps the oxygen rows; all other entries stay unchanged.

A full-headed curved arrow tracks one electron pair from its starting bond or lone pair to its destination. It serves a different purpose from ↔ between complete contributors. [OpenStax's drawing lesson](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/2-6-drawing-resonance-forms) develops these electron-placement patterns.

## Eight diagnostic questions

Find the failed check or explain why each proposal passes. Repair drawings or labels. Parentheses mark branches on the preceding atom.

1. **Missing pair:** H–C(=Oa)–Ob, with LP counts H 0, C 0, Oa 2, Ob 2, is labeled “formate, −1.” Does it contain 18 electrons?
2. **Carbon overload:** H–C(=Oa)=Ob has LP counts H 0, C 0, Oa 2, Ob 2. Its calculated charges sum to −1. Can it represent formate?
3. **Nitrogen overload:** For nitrite, NO₂⁻, propose Oa=N=Ob with LP counts Oa 2, N 1, Ob 2. Both the 18-electron total and −1 charge total pass. What fails?
4. **Nitrate labels:** N(=Oa)(–Ob)(–Oc), with LP counts N 0, Oa 2, Ob 3, Oc 3, is labeled N⁰, Oa⁰, Ob⁻, Oc⁰. Which labels need repair?
5. **Ozone labels:** Oa=Ob–Oc has LP counts Oa 2, Ob 1, Oc 3. Someone labels all three oxygens neutral because O₃ is neutral. Correct the labels.
6. **Hydrogen moved:** Compare worked formate with H–Oa–C=Ob, with LP counts H 0, Oa 2, C 1, Ob 2. It has 18 electrons, a duet at H, octets at C and O, and charge −1. Is it another resonance contributor of formate?
7. **Duplicate drawing:** Compare H–C(=Oa)–Ob⁻ with Ob⁻–C(H)=Oa. Both have LP counts H 0, C 0, Oa 2, Ob 3. Have you found two distinct contributors?
8. **Incomplete octet:** Formaldehyde has two C–H single bonds and C=O, with LP counts C 0, H 0 each, O 2. Move only the C=O π pair onto O. Must the resulting C⁺–O⁻ contributor be rejected?

## Answers and repairs

1. **Only 16 electrons are drawn:** 8 bonding + 8 nonbonding. E values are H 2, C 8, Oa 8, Ob 6; charges are 0, 0, 0, +1. Add a third LP to Ob and label it −1. That restores the worked formate ledger.

2. **Carbon has ten electrons.** Its B = 5 and L = 0 give E = 10 and FC = −1. H has E = 2, FC = 0; each oxygen has E = 8, FC = 0. The total really is 10 bonding + 8 nonbonding = 18. Convert C=Oa to a single bond and give that π pair to Oa. The result is the second worked contributor.

3. **Nitrogen has ten electrons:** B = 4, L = 1, so E = 10 and FC = −1. Each oxygen has E = 8 and FC = 0. Repair by converting N=Ob to N–Ob and adding one LP to Ob. Now N has B = 3, L = 1, E = 8, FC = 0; Ob has B = 1, L = 3, E = 8, FC = −1. Oa stays unchanged. Total: 6 bonding + 12 nonbonding = 18.

4. **N needs +1; Oc needs −1.** N has B = 4, L = 0, E = 8, FC = +1. Oa has B = 2, L = 2, E = 8, FC = 0. Each singly bonded oxygen has B = 1, L = 3, E = 8, FC = −1. The corrected sum is −1, matching nitrate; 8 bonding + 16 nonbonding = 24 electrons.

5. **Ob is +1 and Oc is −1.** Oa has B = 2, L = 2, E = 8, FC = 0; Ob has B = 3, L = 1, E = 8, FC = +1; Oc has B = 1, L = 3, E = 8, FC = −1. Total: 6 bonding + 12 nonbonding = 18. Neutrality constrains the sum, not each atom.

6. **The connectivity changed.** H is attached to Oa instead of C. This drawing has different constitutional connectivity, even though its arithmetic passes: H has E = 2, FC = 0; both oxygens have B = 2, L = 2, E = 8, FC = 0; C has B = 3, L = 1, E = 8, FC = −1. Total: 8 bonding + 10 nonbonding = 18. Electron redistribution alone cannot move H. This comparison tests connectivity; the ledger doesn't establish that the alternative arrangement is a stable species.

7. **One contributor, drawn twice.** C=Oa and C–Ob occur in both; every LP and charge matches the first worked ledger. Reading the chain from the opposite end adds no electron placement. Keeping atom labels prevents this counting mistake.

8. **The minor contributor is valid.** After the move, carbon has three single bonds: B = 3, L = 0, E = 6, FC = +1. Oxygen has B = 1, L = 3, E = 8, FC = −1. Each H has E = 2, FC = 0. Initially C and O each have E = 8, FC = 0. Both forms contain 12 electrons: initially 8 bonding + 4 nonbonding, then 6 + 6. The neutral octet form is more important; the six-electron carbon doesn't make the other form impossible. See the [three-bonded carbon charge pattern](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/2-3-formal-charges) and the [distinction between unequal contributors](https://openstax.org/books/organic-chemistry/pages/2-5-rules-for-resonance-forms).

## Review the error you actually made

Turn a missed question into a repair prompt: “Nitrite totals pass. Which atom is overfilled, and where must one pair go?” Keep the bond and LP specification on the front; put the local calculation and repair on the back.
