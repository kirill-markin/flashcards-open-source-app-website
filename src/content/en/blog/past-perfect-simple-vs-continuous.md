When Imani arrived at the theater at noon, the hallway smelled of paint. Omar, the painter, was already in the lobby. The shift log gave Imani two things to report. Eva **had tested** all six microphones. Omar **had been painting** the stage floor.

Both sentences look back from noon. For the handover, Imani needs to know what she can mark complete.

## What could Imani report at noon?

| Time | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 10:40 a.m. | Omar started painting the stage floor. |
| 11:30 a.m. | Eva tested the last of the six microphones and marked the checks complete. |
| 11:55 a.m. | Omar put down the roller and went to the lobby. The log recorded the time he stopped. |
| Noon | Imani arrived and smelled paint in the hallway. |

“Eva **had tested** all six microphones” gives the director the number of microphones checked by noon. To say how many passed, Imani would need the test results. The past perfect simple often reports what was done by a particular time in the past. [Cambridge’s comparison of the two forms](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/uk/grammar/british-grammar/past-perfect-simple-or-past-perfect-continuous)

“Omar **had been painting** the stage floor” explains the smell Imani noticed. The paint still smelled after Omar stopped. The past perfect continuous, also called the **past perfect progressive**, focuses on the activity leading up to a past moment. It can describe an activity that was still going on then or one whose effects were still noticeable after it stopped. The log tells Imani that Omar stopped at 11:55. She’d need to check how much of the floor he finished. If the whole floor was done, she could report that Omar **had painted** the stage floor. [Cambridge on earlier activity and its effects](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/past-perfect-continuous-i-had-been-doing)

## How to form each tense

Use **had + past participle** for the simple form (*had tested*, *had written*). Use **had been + the -ing form** for the continuous (*had been painting*). *Had* stays the same with every subject. For more practice finding the past moment both forms look back from, see the [past perfect vs past simple timeline lesson](/blog/past-perfect-vs-past-simple/).

## When the duration matters

Either form can describe how long something had lasted by then. “When Imani arrived, Omar **had known** the director for five years.” *Know* describes a state, so the simple form is usual. With verbs like *live*, both forms can work. “When Imani arrived, Omar **had lived** nearby for five years” and “When Imani arrived, Omar **had been living** nearby for five years” give the same duration. The continuous draws attention to the situation over that period. Omar could still live nearby after that day. ## Seven past perfect simple vs continuous exercises

Find the past moment in each example. For a blank, write the whole verb phrase using the past perfect simple or continuous. For the other questions, explain what you can tell from the details you’re given.

1. Dana finished scanning 28 returned books before the library opened. Report the completed count. “By the time the library opened, Dana ___ 28 returned books.” (*scan*)
2. Mei arrived at the bakery at seven and saw flour on Dan’s sleeves. He had put down the rolling pin a few minutes earlier. Focus on the activity that explains the flour. “Dan ___ dough.” (*roll out*)
3. When the bake sale opened, Ana looked tired because she **had been baking** all morning. The stall log says all 40 biscuits were baked and ready before opening. Can both statements be true? Which one establishes that all 40 biscuits were ready?
4. “When the museum closed in 2024, the city ___ the building for 60 years.” (*own*) Can you tell from the sentence who owned the building in 2025?
5. Farah still worked at the clinic at the end of the year. “At the end of the year, Farah ___ at the clinic for six months.” (*work*) Give two forms that fit. What does the continuous draw attention to?
6. At Friday’s meeting, Bo said he **had been cycling** to work every morning that week. Is he describing one ride or a series of rides? Can you tell how he got to work the following Monday?
7. A crew started rewiring a large hall in January. The job was still underway during a February inspection. Describe the work in progress from the inspector’s point of view. “At the time of the inspection, the crew ___ the hall since January.” (*rewire*)

### Answers

1. **Had scanned.** The simple form reports the number of books Dana finished scanning by opening time.
2. **Had been rolling out.** The continuous puts the focus on the activity that left flour on Dan’s sleeves. *Had rolled out* is also possible and focuses on what Dan had done.
3. **Yes. Only the log establishes that all 40 biscuits were ready.** *Had been baking* explains why Ana was tired. It fits whether all the biscuits were ready or some were still in the oven when the sale opened.
4. **Had owned.** *Own* describes a state. The 60 years run up to the museum’s closing in 2024. Who owned the building in 2025 is an open question.
5. **Had worked** or **had been working.** Both give the same duration up to the end of the year. The continuous draws attention to the work that had been going on over those six months.
6. **A series of rides.** *Every morning that week* gives the pattern. Bo could have cycled again the following Monday or taken another way to work.
7. **Had been rewiring.** The scene tells you the work was underway at the inspection. The continuous focuses on the work in progress, and *since January* tells you when it began.
