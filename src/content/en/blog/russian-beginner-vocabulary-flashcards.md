**Магази́н** means a shop or store. Remembering that meaning is one task. Remembering where the stress falls, then finding a useful phrase when you need help, takes a different kind of practice.

A useful Russian vocabulary flashcard makes the recall task clear. This guide uses English instructions, stressed Cyrillic and short original examples. It assumes you can read basic Russian letters, or are learning them alongside a course. You can do the exercises on paper; the [companion beginner Russian deck](/catalog/packages/russian-beginner-everyday-vocabulary/) gives you focused prompts to return to later.

The deck has 96 cards: 72 ask for the English meaning of a Russian word or expression in context, and 24 ask you to produce a selected Russian item from an English cue. Its six groups cover greetings and help, people and family, home, food and shopping, places and directions, and daily activities and time.

## Start with a small situation

Choose something ordinary: greeting someone, finding a shop, asking for help or talking about an object at home. A situation gives you a reason to retrieve the word, and helps you choose which meaning to practise.

Those are reasonable beginner goals. The [Pushkin Institute's elementary-level course](https://pushkininstitute.ru/beginner) includes everyday interaction, introductions, asking for help, directions and reading short practical texts. A vocabulary deck supports part of that work. It doesn't replace the course's listening, pronunciation, grammar or conversation practice.

Here is a small set to learn before trying the questions below:

| Russian word | Meaning here | One short context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **вода́** | water; a feminine noun | **Вода́ холо́дная.** — The water is cold. |
| **ключ** | key for a lock; a masculine noun | **Ключ на столе́.** — The key is on the table. |
| **окно́** | window; a neuter noun | **Окно́ откры́то.** — The window is open. |
| **магази́н** | shop or store; a masculine noun | **Магази́н уже́ откры́т.** — The shop is already open. |

These words and contexts appear in the deck. The gender labels are reference information: if a question asks what *ключ* means, answering **key** completes that task. Save a gender question for a separate attempt when you want to practise it deliberately.

## Keep the stress mark with the word

The acute mark in **вода́** points to the stressed vowel. Say the word with that syllable prominent, rather than treating the mark as decoration. In **окно́** and **магази́н**, stress also falls on the final syllable. A one-syllable word such as **ключ** needs no added mark. These marks are learning aids; ordinary Russian writing usually leaves them out.

Use a reliable dictionary when adding a word. [Akademos](https://orfo.ruslang.ru/search), the Russian Academy of Sciences' normative spelling resource, records these stressed forms. Search for the ordinary spelling without the accent if you need to check an entry. Its interface is in Russian.

Check the form you actually want to use, too. The dictionary form **вода́** becomes **во́ду** in the request **Во́ду, пожа́луйста** — “Water, please.” Both the ending and stress change. The deck shows this distinction beside its dictionary-form prompt for water. You don't need a complete case table to practise that word, but check the ending and stress again when learning a new form.

Stress marks help you practise written forms, but they don't show every detail of pronunciation. Russian vowel reduction, consonant sounds and intonation still need listening and speaking work. The beginner [Между нами unit goals](https://mezhdunami.org/unit01/goal.shtml) treat stress, vowel reduction, Cyrillic and levels of formality as connected parts of learning to read and speak. Text cards give you no listening test or pronunciation feedback.

## Give recognition and production different questions

For recognition, put the Russian word in a short context and ask for its meaning. For production, describe the situation in English and ask for a useful Russian response.

| Task | Prompt | Answer to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognize a word | In **Магази́н уже́ откры́т**, what does **магази́н** mean? | **Shop / store.** |
| Produce a short expression | Someone has helped you. Say “thank you” in Russian. | **Спаси́бо.** |
| Choose an appropriate greeting | Greet an adult stranger politely, using the greeting introduced here. | **Здра́вствуйте.** |

Read **Спаси́бо** and **Здра́вствуйте** before hiding those answers. **Приве́т** is an informal greeting; it suits a different relationship or setting. If formality is what you want to practise, include that setting in the question.

An English cue can have several good Russian answers. A polite daytime greeting could also be **До́брый день**. Treat a model answer as the expression being practised, and check another answer for meaning and register before calling it wrong. For an open prompt such as “Greet someone politely,” insisting on one exact string would test the answer key as much as your Russian.

The deck introduces 12 recognition targets in each practical group before four selected production tasks. At least three other cards separate a word's recognition and production prompts. It doesn't reverse every item automatically: the second direction is included when retrieving that word or expression is useful.

## Notice which part caused the hesitation

After an attempt, identify the thing to correct. If you knew that **магази́н** meant shop but stressed the wrong syllable, keep the meaning success and work on stress. If you remembered **Спаси́бо** only after seeing the first letters, try retrieving the whole expression again after other questions.

For a production answer, writing can expose a spelling gap that a spoken attempt hides. For recognition, explain the target word's meaning without translating every surrounding word. Keep the correction small enough to practise on its own.

Return to the material in later sessions, including items you answered correctly. [Karpicke and Roediger's vocabulary study](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408) found that continued retrieval helped delayed recall. [Cepeda and colleagues' spacing research](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x) also supports spreading learning across occasions, while showing that useful gaps depend on the intended retention period. These findings support regular recall and spaced review; they don't promise a particular result from this deck or prescribe one timetable for everyone.

## Try six mixed questions

Read the words and expressions above first. Then cover the tables and the answers below. These questions practise the material on this page, so you don't need the companion deck to complete them.

1. In **Магази́н уже́ откры́т**, what does **магази́н** mean?
2. Add the stress mark to **вода**.
3. Is **окно́** masculine, feminine or neuter?
4. Greet an adult stranger politely, using the formal “hello” introduced above.
5. Say “thank you” in Russian.
6. In **Ключ на столе́**, what does **ключ** mean?

### Answers

1. **Shop / store.** The complete sentence means “The shop is already open.”
2. **Вода́.** The final vowel is stressed.
3. **Neuter.**
4. **Здра́вствуйте.** For a less constrained real-life greeting, **До́брый день** can also fit the daytime situation.
5. **Спаси́бо.**
6. **Key**, in the sense of a key for a lock. The sentence means “The key is on the table.”

Separate the corrections before another round. A stress mistake, a missing meaning and an unsuitable greeting are different problems. You can practise one without discarding the parts you already recalled.
