In 6/8 time, say **1 la li 2 la li** and tap on the two numbers. Each tap starts a dotted-quarter beat that lasts three eighth-note slots. If a note starts on the first *li* and ties across beat 2, tap while the note is still sounding. If a rest covers a beat, tap through the silence.

## Set up the six-slot count

**Beat 1 starts at slot 1. Beat 2 starts at slot 4.** Use a foot or a free hand to tap. Say all six syllables out loud and keep the space between them even. Open Music Theory uses these same syllables and puts some counts in parentheses when a tie or rest covers a beat. Here, voicing every slot on the first pass helps you keep track of the time during holds and rests. [Open Music Theory on counting compound meter](<https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Open_Music_Theory_2e_(Gotham_et_al.)/01%3A_Fundamentals/1.10%3A_Compound_Meter_and_Time_Signatures>)

```text
Slot    1  2  3  4  5  6
Say     1  la li 2  la li
Tap     X        X
```

With a metronome, set a plain click to 60 per minute for one click per dotted-quarter beat. Say **1** and **2** on alternating clicks and space *la* and *li* evenly between them. To hear every slot, set the click to 180 per minute and say one syllable per click. Your taps still land on the two numbers.

## Try the 6/8 rhythm worksheet

Copy the six-slot grid for each measure. Write `+` where a sound starts, `~` where it keeps sounding, and `.` for silence. A quarter note takes two slots, and a dotted quarter takes three. A tie joins notes of the same pitch into one sound that lasts for their combined values. [Open Music Theory on dots and ties](https://open-musictheory.github.io/docs/fundamentals/rhythmic-values/#dots-and-ties)

- **A.** Quarter note, eighth note, dotted quarter note.
- **B.** Eighth rest, eighth note, eighth note tied to a quarter note, eighth note.
- **C.** Dotted quarter rest, eighth note, quarter note.

1. **Count and tap.** Say every syllable while tapping the two beats. Loop each measure, keeping the same spacing from the last *li* to the next **1**.
2. **Add the rhythm.** Keep tapping and move the count into your head. If you’re tapping with your foot, clap at each `+` to check the starts. To practice the full durations, sing each new note as *dah* on the same pitch. Hold the vowel through any `~` slots and leave the rest slots silent. You can also use an instrument that can hold a note.

### Worked answers

```text
Slot    1  2  3  4  5  6
Say     1  la li 2  la li
Tap     X        X
A       +  ~  +  +  ~  ~
B       .  +  +  ~  ~  +
C       .  .  .  +  +  ~
```

- **A.** Start sounds at slots **1, 3, and 4**. Hold the first sound through slot 2. Give slot 3 one short sound, then hold the dotted quarter from slot 4 through slot 6.
- **B.** Start sounds at slots **2, 3, and 6**. Beat 1 lands in a rest. Tap beat 2 at slot 4 while the sound from slot 3 is still going. Hold it through slot 5, then start a fresh sound at slot 6.
- **C.** Start sounds at slots **4 and 5**. Tap at slot 1 and leave slots 1 through 3 silent. Start a short sound on beat 2, then start the quarter note at slot 5 and hold it through slot 6.

Play B and C back to back. B’s last sound lasts one slot. Your next tap starts C’s silent first beat. Give that rest all three slots, then start C’s first sound on the following tap. If you come in early, go back to counting aloud. Say C’s **1** on the tap, then fit *la* and *li* in before the next tap.

## Feel the difference between 6/8 and 3/4

Both meters fit six eighth-note slots in a measure. With the slots evenly spaced, tap slots **1 and 4** for 6/8. That gives you two dotted-quarter beats with three slots each. Then tap slots **1, 3, and 5** for 3/4. That gives you three quarter-note beats with two slots each. Keep the same eighth-note speed for both passes. [Open Music Theory on grouping beats](https://open-musictheory.github.io/docs/fundamentals/meter/)

If you keep starting the tied note again, turn that specific miss into a flashcard. Ask for the slot where beat 2 falls in measure B and what your voice does there. The answer is **slot 4, keep holding**. Then try the measure again. [More examples of writing music theory flashcards](/blog/music-theory-flashcards/)
