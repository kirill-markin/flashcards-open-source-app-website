Your wrist is distal to your elbow, even when you fold your arm and bring your wrist close to your chest. “Distal means farther away” only helps if you remember the reference: the limb's attachment to the trunk.

Anatomical directional terms describe where one body structure is relative to another. For each comparison, name both structures, choose the reference, then choose the word. The ten terms below use five references: head and feet, front and back, the body's midline, a limb's attachment, and the body surface.

![A tailor bends a denim jacket sleeve so its cuff rests near the shoulder while the order of its parts stays unchanged](/blog/anatomical-directional-terms.png)

## Start with the body in anatomical position

Picture a person standing upright and facing forward, with feet parallel and toes pointing forward. Their arms are beside the body, palms facing forward. Left and right belong to that person; when you face them, their right is on your left.

Use this reference even when a person sits, lies down, or turns a hand. Anatomical descriptions retain the standard orientation rather than following the room or the page. [OpenStax explains anatomical position and this convention](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/1-6-anatomical-terminology).

Try it with a drawing: mark the forehead as superior to the chin, then turn the paper upside down. The relationship stays the same, even though the forehead is now lower on the page.

## The 10 terms, with examples

These are the five pairs to learn first. The reference column is useful when two definitions seem to mean roughly the same thing.

| Term | Reference and meaning | Example comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Superior | Toward the head | The forehead is superior to the chin. |
| Inferior | Toward the feet | The chin is inferior to the forehead. |
| Anterior | Toward the front | The breastbone is anterior to the spine. |
| Posterior | Toward the back | The spine is posterior to the breastbone. |
| Medial | Closer to the body's midline | The nose is medial to the right eye. |
| Lateral | Farther from the body's midline | The right eye is lateral to the nose. |
| Proximal | Nearer the trunk along a limb, toward its attachment | The elbow is proximal to the wrist on the same arm. |
| Distal | Farther along a limb from its attachment | The wrist is distal to the elbow on the same arm. |
| Superficial | Closer to the body surface | The skin over the calf is superficial to the calf muscles. |
| Deep | Farther beneath the body surface | The calf muscles are deep to the skin covering them. |

The first eight definitions follow the [NCI SEER terminology reference](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/body/terminology.html); superficial and deep are also covered in [Open RN's directional terms section](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK607445/). Proximal and distal can describe other structures relative to an origin, but the exercises here use limbs.

Practice reading each comparison backward. If the forehead is superior to the chin, the chin is inferior to the forehead. Keep the two structures and reverse the relationship.

## Proximal vs distal: follow the limb

Sketch one arm as a simple sequence:

**Shoulder → elbow → wrist → fingertips**

Moving along the arm toward the shoulder takes you proximally. Moving toward the fingertips takes you distally. The wrist is distal to the elbow but proximal to the fingertips. In a comparison exercise, “the wrist is distal” needs a second structure so the reader knows what you're comparing.

Now sketch a leg:

**Hip → knee → ankle → toes**

Fill in “The knee is ___ to the ankle.” Trace back toward the limb's attachment: **proximal**. Reverse the sentence: “The ankle is **distal** to the knee.”

Now bend your knee. The ankle remains distal to the knee because their order along the limb hasn't changed. Use that sequence whenever a pose makes straight-line distance misleading.

## Medial vs lateral: find the midline

Draw a line down the center of a front-view body outline. Compare each structure with that line. For example, the right ear is lateral to the right eye; the right eye is medial to the right ear.

This explains why “proximal means closer” and “medial means closer” are incomplete notes. They leave out two different references:

- For proximal, follow the limb toward its attachment.
- For medial, compare positions toward the body's midline.

Consider this faulty explanation: “The ankle is medial to the knee because it is farther along the leg from the hip.” The reason given concerns the limb's attachment, so the appropriate word is **distal**. It doesn't establish anything about distance from the midline. The corrected comparison is “The ankle is distal to the knee on the same leg.”

## More than one description can be correct

In anatomical position, the tip of the nose is both **superior** and **medial** to the right corner of the mouth. Superior compares their positions along the head-to-feet direction. Medial compares their distance from the body's midline. Both descriptions fit the same two landmarks.

So a question asking “Where is A relative to B?” may allow several answers unless it specifies a direction pair. On your own practice cards, include the pair or ask for two relationships explicitly.

Keep depth separate from front and back, too. Skin on your back is superficial to the muscles beneath it, even though it's on the posterior side of your body. These exercises use ordinary human anatomical position; brain-specific and animal orientation conventions need their own references.

## Anatomical directional terms quiz

Cover the table and answer each item with a term and a short reason. Use standard human anatomical position as the reference, including in the questions about a bent arm.

1. Using superior/inferior: the chin is ___ to the forehead.
2. Using anterior/posterior: the breastbone is ___ to the spine.
3. Using medial/lateral: the left ear is ___ to the left eye.
4. Using proximal/distal: the elbow is ___ to the fingertips on the same arm.
5. Using proximal/distal: the ankle is ___ to the knee on the same leg.
6. Using superficial/deep: calf muscles are ___ to the skin covering them.
7. Reverse this comparison: “The right eye is lateral to the nose.”
8. A person folds their arm and places their hand against their shoulder. Does the wrist become proximal to the elbow?
9. Give two directional terms for the tip of the nose relative to the right corner of the mouth: one using the midline and one using head-to-feet position.
10. Repair this explanation: “The wrist is proximal to the elbow because my wrist is near my chest.”

### Answers and reasons

1. **Inferior.** The chin is toward the feet relative to the forehead. The reference is the body's head-to-feet direction.
2. **Anterior.** The breastbone lies toward the body's front relative to the spine. This asks about front/back, not depth beneath skin.
3. **Lateral.** The left ear is farther from the midline than the left eye. Left-sided structures can still be medial or lateral relative to each other.
4. **Proximal.** The elbow is nearer the arm's attachment. Trace from fingertips toward the shoulder to check.
5. **Distal.** The ankle lies farther along the limb from its attachment. Reversing the sentence makes the knee proximal to the ankle.
6. **Deep.** The muscles lie beneath that skin. The reverse comparison makes the skin superficial to the muscles.
7. **The nose is medial to the right eye.** Both structures stay in the sentence; their order and the direction reverse.
8. **No.** The wrist remains distal to the elbow. Folding the arm changes its posture, not the order of structures along it.
9. **Medial and superior.** The tip of the nose is closer to the midline and higher toward the head than that mouth corner. The answers describe different directions.
10. **The wrist is distal to the elbow on the same arm.** Nearness to the chest in a particular pose is the wrong reference; follow the limb from its attachment.

## Turn persistent mistakes into small cards

Use your reason for each quiz answer to decide what needs practice. If you forgot a definition, make a short definition card. If you chose the wrong reference, use a comparison card: “Why is the wrist still distal to the elbow when the arm is folded?” Put the explanation on the back: their order along the limb stays the same.
