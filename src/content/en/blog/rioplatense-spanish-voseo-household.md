*¿Vos tenés la llave?* means “Do you have the key?” If you learned *tú tienes*, the object is familiar but **tenés** may take a moment. Everyday questions at home give you a manageable way to practise Rioplatense Spanish: choose the verb form, identify the household object, and check any pronoun separately.

This guide uses **Buenos Aires, Argentina, as its reference variety**. It is for learners who already know basic Spanish and want to produce *vos* forms in household situations. The explanations are in English, with Russian meanings where they clarify a word or a translation trap. The companion deck has **Russian prompts and explanations with Spanish targets**.

## Start with one regional model

In American Spanish, **voseo** means using *vos* and/or its associated verb forms to address one person. Rioplatense voseo is accepted in educated speech and writing. The pronoun itself does not make an exchange rude; relationships, situation, and tone still matter.

The Buenos Aires model is a useful practice boundary, not a rule for every Argentine or Uruguayan conversation. RAE–ASALE records **tú llegás** in formal registers in Montevideo: a *tú* pronoun with a voseo verb form. Recognize that combination without mixing it into the production model below. See [DPD voseo, §§2.3.1 and 2.4.3](https://www.rae.es/dpd/voseo).

Keep the regional boundary on your practice prompts. The broader [Spanish flashcards guide](/blog/spanish-flashcards/) explains how to record a reference variety and keep a word, its form, and its context together. Here the task is narrower: retrieve a few Buenos Aires forms and household words accurately.

## Compare vos and tú in the present

Start with three regular verbs. Say the *vos* form aloud, then write it with its accent. Each linked DLE entry includes a conjugation table with *tú / vos* forms.

| Infinitive | With tú | With vos | Home example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [hablar](https://dle.rae.es/hablar), speak | hablas | hablás | ¿Vos hablás con tu vecino? — Do you speak with your neighbour? |
| [comer](https://dle.rae.es/comer), eat | comes | comés | ¿Vos comés acá? — Do you eat here? |
| [vivir](https://dle.rae.es/vivir), live | vives | vivís | ¿Vos vivís en esta casa? — Do you live in this house? |

The regular present endings here are **-ás, -és, -ís**. That pattern helps, but changing an accent on a familiar *tú* form will not give you every answer:

| Verb | Tú | Vos | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [tener](https://dle.rae.es/tener) | tienes | tenés | No **ie** in tenés |
| [poder](https://dle.rae.es/poder) | puedes | podés | No **ue** in podés |
| [ser](https://dle.rae.es/ser) | eres | sos | Learn sos as a whole form; it has no written accent |
| [estar](https://dle.rae.es/estar) | estás | estás | The form is identical |

Check the **subject** before choosing a form. In *¿Dónde está el lavarropas?* — “Where is the washing machine?” — the appliance is the subject. Use **está**, even when the person listening is someone you address as *vos*.

Compare that with *¿Dónde tenés la pava?* — “Where do you keep the kettle?” Here you are asking about the listener, so **tenés** is a *vos* form. The household noun does not determine the verb’s person.

## Separate a statement from a request

A present form and an affirmative command do different jobs. Compare **Vos abrís la ventana** — “You open the window” — with **Abrí la ventana** — “Open the window.” For this verb, the final **s** distinguishes the present form from the command. The [abrir table](https://dle.rae.es/abrir) gives both forms; practise the intended job as well as the spelling.

These affirmative commands address one person in the selected model:

| Infinitive | Tú command | Vos command | Household request |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [cerrar](https://dle.rae.es/cerrar) | cierra | cerrá | Cerrá la puerta, por favor. — Close the door, please. |
| [abrir](https://dle.rae.es/abrir) | abre | abrí | Abrí la ventana, por favor. — Open the window, please. |
| [poner](https://dle.rae.es/poner) | pon | poné | Poné el libro en la mesa. — Put the book on the table. |
| [hacer](https://dle.rae.es/hacer) | haz | hacé | Hacé la cama, por favor. — Make the bed, please. |
| [decir](https://dle.rae.es/decir) | di | decí | Decí tu nombre. — Say your name. |

The situation and tone determine how direct a request sounds. You can also ask **¿Podés cerrar la puerta?** — “Can you close the door?” Keep that question separate from *Cerrá la puerta* when practising, so the prompt asks you to choose what you mean.

For prohibitions, this guide and the companion deck use present-subjunctive forms shared with *tú*: **no abras**, **no hagas**, **no pongas**, **no vengas**. Voseante subjunctive variants also occur in Argentina; this is a selected teaching model. RAE–ASALE describes the variation and the preference for tuteante subjunctive forms in educated Rioplatense usage in [DPD voseo, §§2.3.1 and 2.4.3](https://www.rae.es/dpd/voseo).

“Don’t open the window” is therefore **No abras la ventana** in this model. Simply adding *no* to affirmative *abrí* does not produce the prohibition. Compare the imperative and subjunctive rows of [abrir](https://dle.rae.es/abrir).

## Learn the household word with its article and sense

Regional vocabulary overlaps across countries. Learn the word that fits your chosen setting without assuming that every word in the sentence is exclusively Argentinian.

| Word and Russian cue | Meaning here | Boundary to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [la heladera](https://dle.rae.es/heladero) — холодильник | Refrigerator for food | Recorded in Argentina, Uruguay, and several other countries; compare el refrigerador |
| [el lavarropas](https://www.asale.org/damer/lavarropas) — стиральная машина | Washing machine | Masculine in Argentina and Uruguay; compare la lavadora |
| [la pava](https://www.asale.org/damer/pava) — чайник для нагрева воды | Metal vessel for heating water | This sense is recorded for Argentina, Paraguay, and southern Bolivia; the entry does not establish it as a general Uruguayan choice |
| [el repasador](https://www.asale.org/damer/repasador) — кухонное полотенце | Cloth for drying dishes | Recorded in Argentina, Uruguay, and elsewhere; compare el paño de cocina |
| [la canilla](https://www.asale.org/damer/canilla) — водопроводный кран | Water tap | Recorded in several regions, including Argentina and Uruguay; el grifo is another regional choice |
| [la pileta](https://www.asale.org/damer/pileta) — раковина в ванной | Bathroom washbasin | Del baño makes the intended sense clear; pileta can also mean a swimming pool |
| [la frazada](https://dle.rae.es/frazada) — тёплое одеяло | Warm bed blanket | Listed in the DLE without a geographic label; do not treat it as uniquely Argentinian |

In **¿Necesitás una frazada?** — “Do you need a blanket?” — the voseo is in **necesitás**. A household sentence can practise regional grammar while using vocabulary shared more widely.

Keep vocabulary and grammar corrections separate. If a prompt specifically asks for *lavarropas* and you answer *lavadora*, you missed the selected local-word target. *Lavadora* is still valid Spanish.

## Choose the pronoun before checking the accent

Using *vos* does not replace every other second-person form. It goes with **te**, **tu**, and **tuyo/tuya**; after a preposition, use **vos**. Compare *Vos te lavás las manos* — “You wash your hands,” *tu casa* — “your house,” and *con vos* — “with you.” See [DPD voseo §2.1.1](https://www.rae.es/dpd/voseo).

Attached pronouns introduce two decisions: which pronoun refers to the object, and how the whole word is written. Start with an explicit **Spanish** antecedent:

| Spanish object | Components | Vos result | Tú comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| el libro — книга / book | poné + lo | **ponelo**: po-NE-lo | ponlo |
| el vaso — стакан / drinking glass | traé + me + lo | **traémelo**: tra-E-me-lo | tráemelo |

**Lo agrees with the masculine Spanish noun.** [Libro](https://dle.rae.es/libro) is masculine, although Russian *книга* is feminine. Russian «Положи её сюда» can therefore correspond to **Ponelo acá** when the object is **el libro**. Choose the Spanish pronoun from *libro*, not from the gender of its Russian translation.

[Vaso](https://dle.rae.es/vaso) is also masculine. With **el vaso** as the object, **Traémelo** means “Bring it to me”: *lo* identifies the glass and *me* identifies the recipient. The [DPD unstressed-pronoun table](https://www.rae.es/dpd/pronombres%20personales%20%C3%A1tonos) distinguishes masculine singular *lo*, feminine singular *la*, and first-person *me*.

Now check the spelling. Attaching pronouns preserves the verb’s stressed syllable, while the resulting word follows ordinary accent rules; see [DPD tilde §5.3](https://www.rae.es/dpd/tilde). **Ponelo** is stressed on the next-to-last syllable and ends in a vowel, so it needs no written accent. **Traémelo** is stressed on the third syllable from the end, so it takes one. The bare commands [poné](https://dle.rae.es/poner) and [traé](https://dle.rae.es/traer) both have an accent; their attached forms need different spellings.

Some attached forms are shared by *tú* and *vos*. The [imperative of dar](https://dle.rae.es/dar) is **da** for both; adding *me* gives **dame**.

## Try ten small decisions

Cover the tables and answer key. Use the Buenos Aires model above. Each prompt asks for one form, word, or judgment, rather than a full sentence translation.

1. **Present, comer:** “Ты ешь дома? / Do you eat at home?” Complete *¿Vos ___ en casa?*
2. **Local noun, with article:** Give the selected Argentine word for “стиральная машина / washing machine.”
3. **Affirmative command, cerrar:** *___ la puerta, por favor.*
4. **Pronoun:** “Это для тебя / This is for you.” *Es para ___.*
5. **Local noun:** A bathroom washbasin, not a pool: *Limpiá la ___ del baño.*
6. **Present, tener:** Ask the listener where they keep the water-heating kettle: *¿Dónde ___ vos la pava?*
7. **Spelling:** The object is **el libro**. Join *poné + lo*: *___ acá.*
8. **Prohibition, abrir:** Use this guide’s selected subjunctive model: *No ___ la ventana.*
9. **Spelling:** The object is **el vaso**. Join *traé + me + lo*.
10. **Regional judgment:** Does the noun *frazada* itself establish voseo in *¿Necesitás una frazada?* Identify the voseo form.

### Answers and corrections

1. **comés.** *¿Vos comés en casa?* Keep the final stress and written accent. The *tú* form is *comes*.
2. **el lavarropas.** Include the masculine article. *La lavadora* is valid Spanish, but this prompt asks for the selected local word.
3. **Cerrá.** *Cerrá la puerta, por favor.* The *tú* command is *cierra*.
4. **vos.** *Es para vos.* Use *vos* after the preposition in this model.
5. **pileta.** *Limpiá la pileta del baño.* The bathroom context identifies the washbasin sense.
6. **tenés.** *¿Dónde tenés vos la pava?* The explicit subject *vos* makes this a question about the listener. Compare *¿Dónde está la pava?* — “Where is the kettle?” — whose subject is the kettle.
7. **Ponelo.** *Ponelo acá.* *Lo* refers to masculine *el libro*. Stress is po-NE-lo, with no written accent.
8. **abras.** *No abras la ventana.* This is the selected subjunctive form; *no abrí* is not the prohibition.
9. **traémelo.** Stress is tra-E-me-lo. *Me* means “to me”; *lo* refers to masculine *el vaso*. Keep the accent on **é**.
10. **No; necesitás is the voseo form.** *Frazada* names the blanket. Its presence does not identify the address system or make the noun exclusively Argentinian.

Mark the kind of miss: **verb**, **word/article**, **pronoun**, or **spelling**. An incorrect article needs different practice from a missing accent. Keep each correction small enough that you know what you are trying to recall.

## Return to the missed decision later

Read the correction, use the form in a short situation, and try it again after other prompts. Revisit it in a later session too. Recalling an answer before looking gives you a clearer check of what you can produce than recognizing an answer already on the page.

[Karpicke and Roediger’s vocabulary experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408) found that continued retrieval after an initial correct response improved delayed recall. [Cepeda and colleagues’ spacing study](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x/) found that useful review gaps depended on the intended retention period. These results support regular recall and reviews spread across time. They do not establish a universal schedule or demonstrate results for this particular practice set.
