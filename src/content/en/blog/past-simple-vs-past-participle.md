“I went” is correct. “I have went” isn't. You can remember *go, went, gone* and still choose the wrong form in a sentence. Add “did” and you need a different form again: “Did you go?”

The difference between **past simple and past participle** starts with their jobs. Past simple is a tense, as in “I went.” A past participle is a verb form, such as *gone*, used in several grammatical structures. Before reaching for the second or third column of a verb list, look at the sentence around the verb.

## Start with the words around the verb

| Sentence structure | Form needed | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative past simple | Past form | Maya **went** to the library yesterday. |
| Question or negative with did/didn't | Base form | **Did** Maya **go**? She **didn't go**. |
| Perfect structure with have/has/had | Past participle | Maya **has gone** to the library. |
| Passive with a form of be | Past participle | The poster **was drawn** by Maya. |

In “didn't go,” **did** already carries the past tense, so the main verb stays in its base form. The same pattern gives you “Did the lesson begin?” and “They didn't feel ready.” The [British Council's past simple reference](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/past-simple) explains how *did* works in questions and negatives.

Apply that rule when **did is present**. Some past-simple questions have no *did*: “Who led the walk?” The verb *be* also has its own patterns: “Was she ready?”

In perfect structures, *have*, *has*, and *had* act as helping verbs: “They have begun,” “She has written,” “We had eaten.” The main verb takes its past-participle form. An adverb can come between the two: “They have already begun.” The [British Council's participles guide](https://africa.teachingenglish.org.uk/classroom/grammar/participles) explains these combinations and the use of participles in passive sentences.

## A small irregular-verb reference

These seven verbs cover the examples and practice below. For more forms, use the [British Council irregular-verbs list](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/irregular-verbs).

| Base | Past simple | Past participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| begin | began | begun |
| go | went | gone |
| lead | led | led |
| feel | felt | felt |
| write | wrote | written |
| eat | ate | eaten |
| draw | drew | drawn |

Some verbs have three different forms; others share the last two. Compare “I felt cold yesterday” with “I have felt cold all morning.” **Felt** looks identical in both sentences, but the first uses the past simple and the second uses the present perfect.

**Led** is both the past tense and past participle of *lead*, pronounced /led/, as [Cambridge Dictionary confirms](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/led). Write “Nora led the discussion” and “Nora has led three discussions.” The spelling stays the same even though the structure changes.

## Have gone vs went

“My neighbors **went** to the coast last Saturday” places the trip in a finished past period. It doesn't tell us whether they're home now. “My neighbors **have gone** to the coast” says they've left and haven't returned. The [British Council's present perfect reference](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/grammar/english-grammar-reference/present-perfect) explains this use of *have gone*.

Once you've chosen the structure, the verb form follows: **went** works in an affirmative past-simple statement; **gone** follows *have* in the present perfect. “Have went” mixes the two forms.

The name *past participle* doesn't mean the whole sentence must refer to the past. In “The poster **will be drawn** tomorrow,” *drawn* is a past participle, but the action is in the future. This is a passive sentence: someone will draw the poster, and the poster receives the action. The helping verbs and context establish the time.

## Try eight sentences before checking the answers

Choose one form in each sentence. Then name the clue that helped you: an affirmative past statement, *did*, a perfect helping verb, or a passive structure. Try without the table first so you can see where you need practice.

1. Yesterday, our class **(began / begun)** a new project.
2. Did Amir **(go / went)** to the practice session yesterday?
3. We didn't **(ate / eat)** before the train left.
4. The musicians have already **(began / begun)** the second song.
5. When I reached the station, the last train had **(went / gone)**.
6. Last Monday, Lena **(lead / led)** the museum tour.
7. I have **(feel / felt)** more confident since the first lesson.
8. The new mural will be **(drew / drawn)** by local students next month.

### Answers and the reason for each

1. **Began.** This affirmative past-simple statement needs the past form of *begin*. *Begun* is the participle and can't fill this slot on its own.
2. **Go.** After the helping verb *did*, use the base form. “Yesterday” doesn't change that rule.
3. **Eat.** *Didn't* also takes the base form. The sentence is still past simple; *did* carries the tense.
4. **Begun.** *Have* forms the present perfect with a past participle. *Already* sits between the helping verb and participle without changing the pattern.
5. **Gone.** *Had gone* is past perfect. Here, it places the train's departure before my arrival at the station.
6. **Led.** This affirmative past-simple sentence needs the past form of *lead*: *led*. The participle has the same spelling.
7. **Felt.** The present-perfect structure needs a participle after *have*. For *feel*, that form is *felt*, which also serves as the past-simple form.
8. **Drawn.** The mural receives the action in this future passive: *will be + past participle*. “Next month” makes the future time clear; it doesn't change *drawn* into another form.

## Turn the mistake into the right card

If you missed a question, try it again with the verb table visible. Knowing you need a participle but forgetting *begun* is a form-recall problem. Seeing *begun* in the table and still choosing *began* after *have* points to a different task: practicing which form the sentence needs.

Give that choice its own card:

| Mistake | Card front | Card back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Used a past form after did | Did you ___ the invitation? (write) | **write** — did + base form |
| Used a past-simple form after have | The gardeners have ___ work. (begin) | **begun** — have + past participle |
| Forgot the spelling of led | Yesterday, Sam ___ the group upstairs. (lead) | **led** — the past form of lead |
| Assumed participles always mean past time | The menu will be ___ tomorrow. (write) | **written** — future passive: will be + participle |

Keep the helping verb visible when you're practicing how to choose a form. Include the base verb in brackets so you don't have to guess which word the card asks for.
