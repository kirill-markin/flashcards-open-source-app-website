“Lina took the stairs because the elevator was out of service” needs no comma. **Usually, skip the comma before *because* when the reason follows the action it explains.** If a *because* clause starts the sentence, put the comma after the whole clause. [Purdue OWL’s comma rules](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/punctuation/commas/extended_rules_for_commas.html)

> Because the elevator was out of service, Lina took the stairs.

A comma before *because* can mark a reason that the writer adds as an aside. It can also help when a sentence with *didn't* leaves the reader unsure what happened. [MLA Style Center’s guide to commas with *because*](https://style.mla.org/commas-with-because/)

## Did you print the handouts?

> I didn't print the handouts because the schedule had changed.

The sentence could mean you skipped the printing. It could also mean you printed the handouts for some other reason. Before you edit, ask yourself two questions.

1. **Did the action happen?**
2. **Which fact explains the outcome?**

**You skipped the printing.** The schedule change explains why you skipped it. A comma before *because* helps the reader take the whole statement *I didn't print the handouts* as the outcome.

> I didn't print the handouts, because the schedule had changed.

**You printed the handouts.** The instructor had asked for paper copies, and the schedule change played no part in your decision. Readers can easily miss that meaning in the original. State the action and the reason directly, then explain where the schedule change fits.

> I printed the handouts because the instructor had asked for paper copies. The schedule had changed, and that change didn't affect my decision.

You could also move the reason in the original to the front. “Because the schedule had changed, I didn't print the handouts.” That edit only works for the first reading.

If you're reattaching a reason that a period has left on its own, check which action it belongs with. [Sentence fragment practice](/blog/sentence-fragment-practice/)

## Edit each sentence to match the facts

Change only the punctuation in the first three items. Rewrite item 4 and keep every fact from its notes.

1. “Jules booked a smaller room, because three people had canceled.” The point is why Jules chose a smaller room. Keep the booking first.
2. “Because the courtyard was flooded we ate inside.” We ate inside because of the flooding. Keep the reason first.
3. “We didn't open the shop early because the delivery was delayed.” The delivery delay kept us from opening early.
4. “We didn't open the shop early because the delivery was delayed.” The delivery was delayed. We opened early. The commuters' request to open early was our only reason.

### Answers and explanations

1. “Jules booked a smaller room because three people had canceled.” The cancellations explain the booking, so the reason stays attached to the action without a comma.
2. “Because the courtyard was flooded, we ate inside.” The comma after *flooded* marks the end of the introductory clause.
3. “We didn't open the shop early, because the delivery was delayed.” The comma helps show that the delay explains why we didn't open early.
4. “The delivery was delayed. We opened the shop early solely because commuters had asked us to.” The first sentence keeps the fact about the delivery. The second says what we did and gives the commuters' request as our only reason.
