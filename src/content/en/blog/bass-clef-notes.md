The bottom line of the bass clef is G. The space just above it is A. If you learned G–B–D–F–A and then called that first space B, you skipped a position: the mnemonic names only the lines.

**Bass clef lines, from bottom to top, are G–B–D–F–A. The four spaces are A–C–E–G.** Keep those two lists separate, then practice moving between them. The short paper exercise and quiz below will help you check whether you're counting from the correct edge, noticing spaces, and reading the clef before naming the note.

## Find the F between the dots

The bass clef is also called the F clef. Its two dots sit above and below the fourth line from the bottom, marking F. That gives you a place to start even if you forget a mnemonic. [Steps to Music Theory](https://odp.library.tamu.edu/stepstomusictheory/chapter/the-basics/) shows this relationship in its bass-clef lesson.

For these exercises, use natural notes: no sharps, flats, or key signature. Look at the center of the notehead: a line passes through it, or it sits in a space. A stem pointing up or down doesn't change that position.

The numbers below use scientific pitch notation, where middle C is C4. They distinguish notes with the same letter in different octaves. You can first learn the letters, then add the numbers when you're ready to place exact pitches.

## Bass clef notes from bottom to top

Read this table downward to travel **up** the staff. Line and space numbers both start at the bottom.

| Position | Note | Full pitch name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First line, bottom | G | G2 |
| First space | A | A2 |
| Second line | B | B2 |
| Second space | C | C3 |
| Third line, middle | D | D3 |
| Third space | E | E3 |
| Fourth line, between the clef's dots | F | F3 |
| Fourth space | G | G3 |
| Fifth line, top | A | A3 |

The combined sequence is **G2–A2–B2–C3–D3–E3–F3–G3–A3**. Each move to the next line or space advances one letter. Moving upward after G brings you to A; moving downward before A brings you to G.

For the lines, you might use “Good Boys Do Fine Always.” For the spaces, “All Cows Eat Grass” gives A–C–E–G. A sentence can help you reconstruct the answer, but don't spend every practice session reciting from the bottom. Try finding F3 directly, then its neighbors: E3 in the space below and G3 in the space above.

## Where middle C goes

Middle C sits on the **first ledger line above the bass staff**. A ledger line is a short extra line used to extend the staff. There is a space between the top staff line and that first ledger line, so the upward sequence is:

| Position | Pitch |
| --- | --- |
| Top staff line | A3 |
| Space immediately above | B3 |
| First ledger line above | C4, middle C |

Below the staff, count downward: bottom line G2, space immediately below F2, first ledger line below E2. Don't jump straight from a staff line to a ledger line and forget the space between them.

The octave number changes at C, which is why B3 leads to C4. Middle C is the same pitch when written on the first ledger line below a treble staff. [Open Music Theory's octave guide](https://open-musictheory.github.io/docs/fundamentals/pitches/) explains both placements and the numbering convention.

On piano scores, bass clef often appears on the lower staff of the grand staff. Read the actual clef symbol: a note's name isn't determined by which hand happens to play it. The [Open Music Theory notation guide](https://open-musictheory.github.io/docs/fundamentals/basic-natation/) introduces clefs and the grand staff.

## Draw it, check it, then reverse the task

Get a pencil and draw five evenly spaced lines, or use staff paper. This is a written position exercise; you don't need to add stems or rhythm values.

1. Add a bass clef at the start of the staff. Put one dot in the space above the fourth line and the other in the space below it.
2. Hide the tables and the answer paragraph below. Draw six separate noteheads across the staff for **C3, F3, A2, G3, C4, and E2**, in that order. Add ledger lines where needed, keeping the same spacing as the staff lines.
3. Check each note against the tables and ledger-line explanation. Correct the position before writing its name underneath.
4. Cover those names. Start with the last notehead and name all six backward. Point to each notehead as you answer.

Your positions should be: second space, fourth line, first space, fourth space, first ledger line above, first ledger line below. Backward, the names are E2, C4, G3, A2, F3, C3.

If naming your finished drawing feels easy but placing a fresh note doesn't, repeat step 2 on a blank staff with the notes in a different order. Seeing a name and choosing its position needs practice too.

## A mixed bass clef practice quiz

Hide the answers and write your responses first. These questions describe positions in words; they check your staff map rather than how quickly you recognize printed notation. Include octave numbers where requested.

1. Which letter is on the second line from the bottom in bass clef?
2. Which letter is in the second space from the bottom?
3. A notehead sits on the second line from the top. Give its full pitch name.
4. Start at D3 on the middle line. Move up one position, into the neighboring space. What pitch do you reach?
5. A note sits in the space immediately above the top line. Is it B3 or C4?
6. Where would you draw middle C on a bass staff?
7. What pitch is on the first ledger line below the bass staff?
8. A bottom-line note is labeled E4. The staff has a bass clef. Is the label correct?
9. Someone asks you to “draw G in bass clef.” Which two positions within the five-line staff could they mean?

### Answers and what to check

1. **B.** Counting G–B starts with the bottom line. A is the space between them.
2. **C.** Count spaces separately: A, then C.
3. **F3.** Second from the top is fourth from the bottom. Starting a bottom-to-top mnemonic at the top reverses the map.
4. **E3.** F3 is the next line, two staff positions above D3. Getting F here means you skipped the space.
5. **B3.** C4 needs the ledger line above that space.
6. **On the first ledger line above.** Draw the short line through the notehead, not below it.
7. **E2.** Going down from G2 passes through the F2 space before reaching that ledger line.
8. **No: it is G2.** E4 names the bottom line in treble clef. Check the clef before applying a familiar pattern.
9. **G2 on the bottom line, or G3 in the fourth space.** The letter alone doesn't specify which octave to draw.

Choose your next exercise from the mistake. For line/space confusion, draw D3–E3–F3 across a fresh staff. For direction errors, point to the bottom before counting. For clef confusion, name the clef aloud before each note. For octave mistakes, write B2–C3 and B3–C4 beside their staff positions.
