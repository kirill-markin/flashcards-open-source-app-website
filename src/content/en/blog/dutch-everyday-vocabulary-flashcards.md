You can recognize *raam* as “window” and still hesitate when you need **het raam** or **de ramen**. Dutch vocabulary flashcards become more useful when they expose that difference: which word you need, which article belongs with it, and how its plural is written.

This practice guide is for English-speaking beginners who can already follow short Dutch sentences. Start with one familiar situation: doing something at home, contacting a colleague, or arranging a family day. The tables and self-check below work on paper; the companion deck uses English instructions and Dutch answers.

## Choose a situation you can picture

Choose words for something you might actually do: clean a room, send a message, or collect a child. Here are three starting sets drawn from the companion deck. Read each row as a scene, then practise its nouns and verbs separately.

| Situation | Nouns to learn with their forms | Actions to practise | A sentence to understand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open a window and clean a table | **het raam → de ramen** — window; **de tafel → de tafels** — table, the furniture | **openen → ik open** — open; **schoonmaken → ik maak … schoon** — clean | *Ik maak de tafel schoon.* — I clean the table. |
| Contact a colleague about a message | **de collega → de collega's** — colleague; **het bericht → de berichten** — message | **bellen → ik bel** — phone; **sturen → ik stuur** — send | *Ik stuur mijn collega een bericht.* — I send my colleague a message. |
| Take a child somewhere, then collect them | **het kind → de kinderen** — child; **het gezin → de gezinnen** — family household, typically with children | **brengen → ik breng** — take or bring to a destination; **halen → ik haal** — fetch or collect | *Ik breng mijn kind naar school.* — I take my child to school. |

These sets cover a few useful situations. They aren't a complete beginner vocabulary list. If you don't have children, use the family row to understand someone else's plans, or start with the home or work row.

The noun forms can be checked in the Algemeen Nederlands Woordenboek entries for [raam](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/raam), [tafel](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/tafel), [collega](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/collega), [bericht](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/bericht), [kind](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/kind), and [gezin](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/gezin). For *kinderen*, [Taaladvies.net also uses the singular/plural pair explicitly](https://taaladvies.net/01-minuten-of-01-minuut/).

## Recall the word and its article together

English “the” doesn't tell you whether a Dutch singular noun needs *de* or *het*. Learn **het raam** as your answer to “window,” rather than learning *raam* now and postponing the article.

Dutch uses *het* with neuter singular nouns and *de* with masculine and feminine singular nouns. The definite article for plural nouns is **de**, including plurals of *het*-words. [Taaladvies.net's article reference](https://taaladvies.net/termen-lidwoord/) explains this distinction. So *het raam → de ramen* changes the article, while *de tafel → de tafels* keeps *de*.

Make two focused questions from a noun entry:

| Recall task | Prompt | Target answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Produce the word with its singular article | Window: give the Dutch noun with **de** or **het** | **het raam** |
| Produce the complete plural | Plural of **raam**, meaning window: include the definite article | **de ramen** |

The first task asks you to retrieve both the word and its article. If you remember *raam* but say *de raam*, record an article error. If no Dutch word comes to mind, record a word error. The plural task gives you the singular noun, so you only need to retrieve the plural. Write that answer down: saying it aloud won't show whether you remembered its spelling.

Suppose you answer *het raam* correctly, then write *de raamen*. Keep the first success and correct the second answer to **de ramen**. Learn the whole written plural instead of recording only “add -en.” Other words behave differently: *tafel → tafels*, *bericht → berichten*, and *kind → kinderen*.

For a table you eat or work at, practise **tafels**. A dictionary may also show *tafelen*, but [the WikiWoordenboek entry distinguishes that form by sense](https://nl.wiktionary.org/wiki/tafel#Nederlands); it isn't the furniture plural targeted here. For colleagues, **collega's** is the usual plural. *Collegae* is also possible, though very formal, as [Team Taaladvies explains](https://www.vlaanderen.be/team-taaladvies/taaladviezen/collega-collegas-collegae).

## Give a verb enough context to choose its meaning

A bare English cue can be ambiguous. “Call” might mean phoning someone or saying what their name is. Use **phone a colleague** when practising *bellen*. The sentence tells you which action to retrieve.

The same care helps with *halen* and *brengen*. Compare two original practice sentences:

- **Ik breng mijn kind naar school.** — I take my child to school. The child goes to the destination with my help.
- **Ik haal mijn kind van school.** — I collect my child from school. I go to get the child.

These are useful meanings in this situation, rather than exhaustive translations of either verb. English “take” and “bring” also depend on the speaker's viewpoint; don't force a one-to-one translation for every sentence. The Dutch dictionary entries for [halen](https://nl.wiktionary.org/wiki/halen#Nederlands) and [brengen](https://nl.wiktionary.org/wiki/brengen#Nederlands) give their wider uses.

For each selected verb, distinguish two tasks. First, read a sentence such as *Ik stuur mijn collega een bericht* and explain what *stuur* means there: **send**. Later, hide the verb and complete *Ik ___ mijn collega een bericht* using the cue “send.” The model answer is **stuur**. Understanding the sentence and producing the form are separate attempts. If you supply the infinitive *sturen* as well, the task focuses on forming **ik stuur**; it no longer checks whether you can choose the Dutch verb from English alone.

Keep the answer limited to the requested form. With *schoonmaken*, however, that form has two parts in these main clauses: **Ik maak de tafel schoon.** A gap exercise needs **Ik ___ de tafel ___**, with **maak … schoon** as the answer. [ANW records both parts of this present-tense form](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/schoonmaken).

Family words need a sense cue too. **De familie** can include your wider relatives. **Het gezin** focuses on the family as a household, typically with children. The meanings overlap: *familie* isn't restricted to distant relatives, and a *gezin* doesn't always have children. [ANW explicitly notes childless uses of gezin](https://anw.ivdnt.org/article/gezin).

## Run a short round, then return later

Read the chosen set and its examples before hiding anything. Start with enough material to understand the questions; guessing unfamiliar Dutch from an English cue isn't a useful first encounter.

Then cover the answers and try the requested word or form. Say noun-and-article answers aloud if you like; write the plurals to check their spelling. Check after each attempt. Note the part to correct: **word**, **article**, **plural spelling**, or **verb form**. Try the missed item again after other questions, and keep a noun's article and plural questions apart so that one answer doesn't immediately cue the next.

Return in a later session, including words you answered correctly. Repeated retrieval can support longer-term retention: [Karpicke and Roediger's vocabulary experiment](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408) found a delayed-recall benefit from continuing to test already-recalled words. Spacing reviews across sessions also matters, but the useful gap depends on how long you want to retain the material, as [Cepeda and colleagues' spacing experiment](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x) shows. Neither study establishes a guaranteed result or a universal review timetable for this deck.

For a broader guide to choosing focused prompts and manageable review material, see [how to use flashcards for language learning](/blog/how-to-use-flashcards-for-language-learning/).

## Try eight mixed questions

Cover the earlier tables and the answers below. Recall the words introduced on this page; the noun answers are the targets from these sets, rather than every possible translation of the English cue. For a **noun**, give the Dutch singular with *de* or *het*. For a **plural**, write *de* plus the complete plural. Verb gaps ask for the indicated verb's present **ik** form; use the supplied infinitive.

1. **Noun:** message.
2. **Plural:** kind — child.
3. **Verb:** use *openen*, “open”: *Ik ___ het raam.*
4. **Meaning:** what does **bel** mean here? *Ik bel mijn collega.*
5. **Noun:** family household, typically with children.
6. **Plural:** collega — colleague.
7. **Verb:** use *schoonmaken*, “clean”: *Ik ___ de tafel ___.*
8. **Verb:** use *halen*, “collect”: *Ik ___ mijn kind van school.*

### Answers and corrections

1. **Het bericht** — message. Remember *het* with the singular noun.
2. **De kinderen** — the children. Learn the complete plural; simply adding *-en* to *kind* doesn't produce it.
3. **Open:** *Ik open het raam.* — I open the window. The requested form is *open*, not the infinitive *openen*.
4. **Phone / call on the phone:** *Ik bel mijn collega.* — I phone my colleague. The context identifies a phone call.
5. **Het gezin** — the family as a household. *De familie* can also mean family, including wider relatives; *gezin* is the household-focused word practised here.
6. **De collega's** — the colleagues. Keep the apostrophe. **De collegae** is also correct, though very formal.
7. **Maak … schoon:** *Ik maak de tafel schoon.* — I clean the table. Both gaps belong to the same separable verb.
8. **Haal:** *Ik haal mijn kind van school.* — I collect my child from school. This is collecting the child, rather than taking the child to school.

An error here tells you what to revisit. It doesn't measure your overall Dutch level. Once the forms are familiar, change a small detail in a sentence and try using it in a real exchange. Listening, pronunciation, and conversation need practice beyond these written tasks.
