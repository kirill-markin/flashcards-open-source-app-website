Yes, Quizlet has autosave for private drafts, though a connection failure can leave terms unsaved. Look under **Your library → Flashcard sets → In Progress** where you created the set: drafts don't sync between website and apps. See Quizlet's [draft instructions](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029768912-Editing-draft-sets).

If your terms are still on screen, copy them somewhere else before troubleshooting. A missing set and a visible set that hasn't finished saving need different next steps.

## Start with what you can still see

Choose the situation closest to yours:

| What happened | What to do first |
| --- | --- |
| The editor still shows your terms | Preserve the text using the checklist below before navigating away. |
| The editor is closed and the draft seems missing | Return to the original device and website or app, then check the draft location above. |
| You're unsure which account you used | Compare the displayed username with your usual account details. Don't log out of the mobile app to investigate an unpublished draft. |
| You discarded the draft or logged out of the mobile app | [Discarded drafts can't be restored; mobile logout discards unpublished drafts](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029768912-Editing-draft-sets). Look for your own source copy. |
| You deleted a finished set | Quizlet says [deleted sets can't be recovered](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030171011-Deleting-a-set). Gather any notes or saved copies for rebuilding. |

The linked product instructions were checked on September 20, 2026; they weren't tested in a logged-in account.

An empty list alone doesn't establish that you deleted anything. Write down what you remember: the set title, account, device, whether you used a browser or app, and the last action before it disappeared. This gives you a short search plan instead of a cycle of opening the same screens.

## If your Quizlet set isn't saving

Quizlet's [saving guidance](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029768912-Editing-draft-sets) describes warnings beside unsaved terms and an autosaved count at the top. Keep the page open while reconnecting.

While you have the editor open:

1. Copy the terms and their complete definitions into a separate document or two spreadsheet columns. Start with any visibly flagged pairs.
2. Check the pasted result. A term without its answer leaves you with more reconstruction later.
3. Save that document and confirm you can read it outside the editor. Text left only on the clipboard is easy to overwrite.
4. If copying isn't practical, capture readable screenshots, including long answers that need scrolling. Check that no text is cut off.
5. Record the expected card count and anything you couldn't preserve, such as an image or audio explanation.

This is a manual preservation step. It doesn't restore a missing draft or prove that Quizlet received your latest edits.

### You wrote 30 cards, but only 27 are autosaved

Suppose your vocabulary set has 30 visible pairs. The counter reports 27 saved, and the last three pairs carry warnings. This is an example, not a claim about how often saving fails.

Copy those three complete pairs first, then preserve the rest if practical. In your separate record, write:

```text
Set: Spanish travel vocabulary
Expected: 30 pairs
Visible saving status: 27
Copied separately: all 30 pairs
Need to check: final 3 pairs and today's correction to card 12
```

The note about card 12 matters. A matching total can't tell you whether an earlier definition contains your latest correction. After saving succeeds, compare both the total and the wording you changed. If you later find only part of the set, compare it with your copy and restore the missing material rather than retyping everything.

## Finish the set with its visibility in mind

To [publish the draft](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029768912-Editing-draft-sets), select **Create** on the website or **Done** in the app. Published sets are available across devices.

Check visibility before publishing study material you intend to keep private. Quizlet says sets are generally public by default, with age-based exceptions. To keep a set private, choose **Just me** in its visibility settings. Follow Quizlet's [visibility instructions](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-ca/articles/360030255191-Changing-a-set-s-visibility) for your platform.

## Keep enough source material to rebuild

For your next writing session, keep the term-definition pairs in one saved document and add a small record alongside them:

- A recognizable set title and the account used.
- The date and expected number of pairs.
- The lecture, chapter, or page numbers behind the cards.
- Any unfinished answers, clearly marked for review.

Source references are useful when the wording itself is gone. They narrow the rebuilding job to a few pages instead of an entire course. If you reconstruct an answer from memory, mark it as unverified until you've compared it with the teaching material.

Quizlet supports [importing prepared terms and definitions on its website](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029977151-Creating-sets-by-importing-content). Our [Quizlet import guide](/blog/how-to-import-flashcards-into-quizlet/) covers rebuilding from two columns. Right now, save the wording you can still access. Even a partial copy can spare you from rewriting answers you've already worked out.
