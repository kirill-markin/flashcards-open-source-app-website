Suppose your chapter has 80 flashcards, but only six keep slowing you down. Before putting all six into another review session, check why each one is difficult. A forgotten answer needs practice. An ambiguous question needs editing. Repeating both twenty times won't solve the same problem.

Use a small revision list with a clear purpose: practice the answers you couldn't produce, check that the intended cards enter the session, then return to the wider chapter. Here's how to set that up with Quizlet starred terms.

![A climbing instructor marks a few blue holds with yellow tape while the rest of the practice wall stays visible](/blog/quizlet-starred-terms.png)

## Choose the starred terms in Quizlet

[Quizlet's instructions for studying with stars](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360031172312-Studying-most-missed-terms-with-stars), checked October 1, 2026, describe this process:

1. Sign in and open your set.
2. Select the star beside each term you want to study.
3. Choose **Starred**, or **Study X** beside **Study all**, depending on the interface shown.
4. Choose a review activity to start the selected session.

Select a star again to remove it. Your stars are private; other people can't see your selection.

For Flashcards, the help page also describes opening **Options**, turning off **Sorting**, and choosing **Restart Flashcards** to star terms. Treat this as a documented control sequence, not a general fix for missing cards.

For stars across several sets, Quizlet documents adding the sets to a folder, opening an activity, and selecting **Options → Study starred**.

These are documentation-based instructions. The worksheet and checks below are a suggested study routine, not automatic Quizlet features.

## Give each difficult card a reason

Before a focused session, answer a few cards without revealing their backs. Write the answer, say it aloud, or commit to it mentally before checking. Record what happened rather than whether the answer looked familiar afterward.

Use this six-card worksheet as a model. These are hypothetical cards from an English-course set containing vocabulary and course reminders. Replace them with your own prompts and observations.

| Card and prompt | What happened | Action before the next review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: “I ___ your invitation: accept or except?” | Wrong answer: you said “except.” | Include it. Note that “accept” means agree to receive; “except” means excluding. |
| B: “May I ___ your pen: borrow or lend?” | Guessed answer: you picked “borrow” but couldn't explain why. | Include it. Recall the word without choices and explain who receives the pen. |
| C: “What is the plural of child?” | Correct recall: you said “children” before looking. | Leave it outside this round; revisit it in the wider review. |
| D: “What does light mean?” | Unclear prompt: you answered “not heavy”; the back says “illumination.” | Add context, such as “This bag is light.” Check the revised answer before practicing. |
| E: “Which word list is on Friday's quiz?” | Outdated fact: the back says Unit 3; the teacher's revised handout says Unit 4. | Check the latest handout and correct the reminder before using it to plan revision. |
| F: “What is the past participle of write?” | Outside scope: “written” belongs to a later verbs unit, excluded from this quiz. | Leave it outside this round; note the later unit where it belongs. |

For this example, your immediate practice list contains A and B. D and E have separate repair tasks. C and F stay outside the session for different reasons: one was recalled successfully, while the other doesn't serve today's goal.

Keep a short note such as “D: add a sentence using light” or “E: check revised quiz handout.” If you can't edit the set, keep the corrected wording in your own source notes and practice from those. For help narrowing vague questions, see [how to make better flashcards](/blog/how-to-make-better-flashcards/).

## Check one included card and one excluded card

Before investing a full session, write down two recognizable examples: A, which belongs in your focused list, and C, which should stay outside it. Also note the expected list: A and B, two cards.

Check the selected terms against that note before starting. A matching count helps, but two wrong cards would also make a list of two. Confirm the identities as well.

During practice, look for A, the invitation question, and watch for C, the plural of “child.” Seeing C is a reason to stop and inspect the selection. Not seeing A immediately proves little; it may simply appear later. Seeing A while C hasn't appeared confirms only that A reached practice. It doesn't establish that every intended card is included or every unwanted card is excluded.

For this two-card example, keep a tally of whether both A and B appear. With a larger list, compare each intended card against what you actually reviewed before claiming complete coverage. A short session can leave that question unresolved.

If your Quizlet starred terms aren't showing as expected, separate two questions:

- Does the selected list contain the cards you intended?
- Does the activity you're using actually present those cards?

If the first check fails, correct the selection before continuing. If the list is right but the activity still behaves unexpectedly, record the set, activity, device, and one concrete included or excluded example for support. Avoid treating an unexpected session as evidence that you've forgotten more material.

## Finish with a way back to the chapter

Work through A and B, inspect each mistake, and try again after doing something else. An immediate repeat can tell you whether you understood a correction; a later attempt gives you another chance to produce it without the answer still in view.

Set an exit rule for this round: when you can answer a card without hints and explain the point you previously missed, move it out of the immediate practice list. Keep it if you're still guessing. This is a practical stopping rule, not proof of permanent mastery.

Before closing your notes, schedule a broader pass through the chapter. Include C again, bring repaired cards back into practice, and check the remaining material for fresh gaps. Keep F's separate scope note so it doesn't get mistaken for something you've already learned.

Leave yourself one concrete next step: “Tomorrow, answer the chapter cards without hints; rebuild the short list from the misses.” Your revision list should change with what you can actually recall.
