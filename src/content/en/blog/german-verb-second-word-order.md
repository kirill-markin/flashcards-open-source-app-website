*Morgen ich arbeite* has familiar words in the wrong order for a standard German statement. Write **Morgen arbeite ich**: “Tomorrow I'm working.” Starting with *morgen* uses the first position, so *arbeite* follows it. Keeping *ich* before the verb would leave two separate elements in front of the finite verb.

German verb-second word order gets easier to check when you mark phrases instead of counting words. The practice below uses a phrase ledger, eight exercises, and an explained answer key to help you find exactly where an order went wrong.

## Second position can follow several words

The **finite verb** is the conjugated verb form: *ich kaufe*, *du kaufst*, *Lara kauft*. The [Leibniz Institute for the German Language defines a V2 clause](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/terminologie/939) by this verb's second position.

The **German V2 rule** puts one complete sentence element before the finite verb in the statements practiced here. A one-word subject, a longer noun phrase, or a whole time or place phrase can fill that first position:

| First element | Finite verb | Rest | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lara | kauft | heute auf dem Markt Brot. | Lara is buying bread at the market today. |
| Heute | kauft | Lara auf dem Markt Brot. | Today, Lara is buying bread at the market. |
| Auf dem Markt | kauft | Lara heute Brot. | At the market, Lara is buying bread today. |

*Auf dem Markt* contains three words but fills one position. Keep that phrase together; *auf* alone doesn't express the intended place.

These sentences describe the same purchase, but their openings frame the information differently. Fronting a phrase can change the topic or emphasis. You aren't learning that every order sounds identical in every conversation.

The scope here is **standard written declarative clauses**. Questions, commands, and conversational constructions need their own treatment.

## Build a phrase ledger before moving anything

Use the first sentence above as your starting inventory:

| Piece | Job | Keep together? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lara | Subject: who buys? | Yes |
| kauft | Finite verb | Reserve position two |
| heute | Time: when? | Yes |
| auf dem Markt | Place: where? | Yes, all three words |
| Brot | Object: what does she buy? | Yes |

To start with the place, write *Auf dem Markt*, then *kauft*, then the remaining pieces in this order: *Lara heute Brot*. Mark the result like this:

**[Auf dem Markt] [kauft] [Lara] [heute] [Brot].**

The brackets show why *kauft* is in second position even though it's the fourth word. You haven't added a word, changed a verb ending, or lost the buyer. Don't put a comma between the opening place phrase and the verb.

In these examples, the subject follows the finite verb when another phrase comes first. That's a useful pattern to practice here. **“The subject must always be immediately after the verb” is not a universal German rule.** This lesson doesn't teach every possible ordering of the material after the verb.

## Find the finite verb when the verb has several parts

The verb carrying the main action isn't always the finite one. Compare these three original examples:

| Sentence | Finite verb | Verb material at the end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heute **muss** ich Brot **kaufen**. | muss | kaufen: infinitive, “to buy” |
| Heute **habe** ich Brot **gekauft**. | habe | gekauft: past participle, “bought” |
| Heute **rufe** ich Lara **an**. | rufe | an: separable prefix of anrufen, “to call” |

The meanings are “Today I have to buy bread,” “Today I bought bread,” and “Today I'm calling Lara.” In each, the bold parts form a frame around the middle of the clause. [Dartmouth's word-order guide](https://germanstudiesdepartmenaluser.host.dartmouth.edu/WordOrder/WordOrder.html) explains this split between the finite verb and its infinitive, participle, or separable prefix.

For the modal example, *muss* agrees with *ich*; *kaufen* stays an infinitive. Changing to *du* gives *Heute musst du Brot kaufen*. The [University of Texas modal-verb reference](https://www.coerll.utexas.edu/gg/gr/vm_01.html) supplies these forms and the modal-plus-infinitive pattern.

## Check the clause boundary

Check each clause separately; a sentence can contain more than one finite verb. In this standard written example, *weil* (“because”) introduces a dependent clause that gives the reason for staying home:

**Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich heute arbeiten muss.**  
“I'm staying at home because I have to work today.”

The main clause has *ich → bleibe*. Inside the *weil* clause, *arbeiten muss* comes at the end, with finite *muss* last. This is a different structure from *Heute muss ich arbeiten*.

Now move that entire reason to the front:

**Weil ich heute arbeiten muss, bleibe ich zu Hause.**

For the main clause's V2 pattern, **the whole opening dependent clause fills the first position**. *Bleibe* follows the comma, then *ich*. Writing *Weil ich heute arbeiten muss, ich bleibe zu Hause* would put the reason clause and the main-clause subject before *bleibe*. The [University of Texas word-order lesson](https://www.coerll.utexas.edu/gg/gr/con_06.html) explains both clause orders. Use this *weil* pattern for the exercises without assuming every dependent construction behaves identically.

Connecting two main clauses works differently. In *Ich lerne Deutsch, aber ich arbeite heute*, each side of *aber* (“but”) has its own V2 pattern. Connecting *aber* sits outside the second clause's position count; *ich* fills position one and *arbeite* fills position two. The [University of Michigan's guide](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/german-resources/grammatik/wortstellung/) calls this conjunction slot “position 0.”

## A reusable sentence audit

Copy this onto paper beside an exercise:

```text
Required opening: ____________________
Clause type: _________________________
Complete first element: ______________
Finite verb and its subject: _________
Remaining phrases: ___________________
Verb material at the end, if any: _____
Finished sentence: ____________________
```

First identify each clause and its finite verb. For a V2 statement, check that exactly one complete element precedes that verb, excluding a connecting conjunction such as *aber*. Then account for every remaining phrase and any infinitive, participle, or separable prefix. For the written *weil* clauses here, check the clause-final verb instead.

This gives you a diagnosis more useful than “the answer looks wrong”: two elements before the verb, a split place phrase, a missing prefix, or the wrong clause pattern.

## Eight German word order exercises

Keep all supplied words and verb forms. Change capitalization and punctuation as needed. For 1–5, start with the required opening; don't add another copy of it. Write your answers before reading the key. The answers are models: check your opening, verb placement, retained words, and intended meaning rather than requiring an exact text match.

1. **Start with Am Morgen:** *Wir trinken am Morgen Tee.* (“We drink tea in the morning.”)
2. **Start with In der Küche:** *Ich koche heute in der Küche Suppe.* (“I'm cooking soup in the kitchen today.”)
3. **Start with Heute:** *Ich muss heute Brot kaufen.* (“I have to buy bread today.”)
4. **Start with Um acht Uhr:** *Lena ruft um acht Uhr ihren Bruder an.* (“Lena calls her brother at eight o'clock.”)
5. **Start with Gestern:** *Paul hat gestern das Buch gekauft.* (“Paul bought the book yesterday.”)
6. **Repair only the weil clause:** *Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich muss heute arbeiten.* (“I'm staying at home because I have to work today.”)
7. **Start with the whole Weil clause:** *Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich heute arbeiten muss.* Keep the reason clause intact and move it before the main clause.
8. **Repair both statements; keep Heute and morgen as their openings:** *Heute ich lerne Deutsch, aber morgen ich arbeite.* (“Today I'm learning German, but tomorrow I'm working.”)

## Answers and what to check

1. **Am Morgen trinken wir Tee.** *Am Morgen* is one time phrase. Finite *trinken* follows it; *wir* is still the subject. The fact that *trinken* also looks like an infinitive doesn't make it one here.
2. **In der Küche koche ich heute Suppe.** The full place phrase fills position one. *Koche* follows *Küche*, even though it is the fourth written word.
3. **Heute muss ich Brot kaufen.** Finite *muss* takes position two. *Kaufen* stays at the end; it doesn't move beside *heute*.
4. **Um acht Uhr ruft Lena ihren Bruder an.** Keep the three-word opening intact and retain final *an*. Removing it changes *anrufen*, the supplied verb.
5. **Gestern hat Paul das Buch gekauft.** *Hat* is finite; *gekauft* is the participle. You move the time phrase without converting the sentence to a different tense.
6. **Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich heute arbeiten muss.** The main clause was already correct. Inside the *weil* clause, move *muss* after *arbeiten*.
7. **Weil ich heute arbeiten muss, bleibe ich zu Hause.** The opening clause stays intact, including final *muss*. Main-clause *bleibe* follows the comma, then *ich*.
8. **Heute lerne ich Deutsch, aber morgen arbeite ich.** Audit each statement separately: *Heute → lerne* and *morgen → arbeite*. Don't count connecting *aber* as the first element of the second statement.

German allows other valid orders, particularly when emphasis changes. A different sentence can also be grammatical yet miss an exercise's constraint: *Ich muss heute Brot kaufen* is correct German, but it doesn't start with *Heute* as exercise 3 requires. If you're unsure about a different order after the finite verb, check it separately; V2 alone doesn't determine the order of everything that follows.

## Keep only the review cards you need

If you missed a pattern, turn it into one focused prompt. Paper or any flashcard app works. These three cards are optional:

| Front | Back |
| --- | --- |
| Start with *Am Morgen*: Wir trinken am Morgen Tee. | Am Morgen trinken wir Tee. *Am Morgen* fills one position. |
| Complete with *muss* and *kaufen*: Heute ___ ich Brot ___. | Heute muss ich Brot kaufen. Finite modal second; infinitive at the end. |
| Repair: Weil ich heute arbeiten muss, ich bleibe zu Hause. | Weil ich heute arbeiten muss, bleibe ich zu Hause. The opening clause fills position one. |

Use the mistake to choose the card. If you dropped *an* in exercise 4, make a prefix card instead of collecting three prompts you already answer easily.
