Your biology midterm is three weeks away. Some lecture cards already have review history, others haven't been practiced, and one final lecture hasn't happened yet. A useful RemNote exam study plan has to make room for all three without pulling next month's chapter into today's workload.

Start with the documents the exam actually covers. Then check whether the projected practice fits your time, and schedule the final lecture when its cards arrive. The walkthrough below follows RemNote's current documentation; the biology example and its numbers are hypothetical.

![A gardener checks space for a new seedling beside established plants and a separate tray of younger seedlings](/blog/remnote-exam-scheduler.png)

## Give the exam a precise boundary

The October 26 midterm covers cell structure, membranes, and enzymes. Create an exam-only folder named **Biology — Midterm 1** with those lecture documents. Keep **Chapter 7 — Genetics**, which belongs to the next test, outside it. You can also select existing documents directly rather than reorganizing your notes.

Count actual flashcards rather than note headings. If a question has two practice directions, count each direction separately:

| Material | Unique cards now | Included in this exam? |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Cell structure | 90 | Yes |
| Membranes | 80 | Yes |
| Enzymes | 70 | Yes |
| Genetics | 60 | No |
| Final lecture | Not created yet; estimate 40 | Intended, once added and scheduled |

The exam currently contains **240 unique cards**. Those four existing units contain 300 altogether, but the extra 60 don't belong to this test. The expected exam total becomes 280 if the final lecture contributes 40 relevant cards. Keep that estimate separate from cards that exist today.

Open **Flashcards → Exams → Create Exam** and choose the documents or folder. A selected folder includes nested material, so inspect its contents. Set the date, say whether more cards are coming, choose how to schedule unseen cards, and review the projected goal. Setup also offers study-day preferences and an optional final review. Creating an exam preserves previous practice history. [RemNote's exam setup guide](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9101991-preparing-for-an-exam) explains these controls.

Suppose 160 of the 240 cards have been practiced and 80 are unseen. Keep the 160 cards' history: it gives the scheduler information about how you answered them. There's no reason to reset those cards just to start the exam plan.

The current [pricing table](https://www.remnote.com/pricing) lists one exam on Free and unlimited exams on Pro. Check that limit if you're planning several tests.

## Write down what the counters mean

A unique card is one question you can practice. A practice event is one attempt at answering it. One card can produce several events before exam day, especially when you forget it. A card you've seen once is no longer unseen, but may still need substantial practice.

Copy this ledger into a note and fill the last column from your exam. Date the entry so you can compare it with the plan after the next lecture.

| Record | Biology example or instruction | Your exam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current scope | 240 existing unique cards; Genetics excluded | ___ |
| Previously practiced / unseen | 160 / 80 unique cards at setup | ___ |
| Expected additions | About 40 cards from the final lecture | ___ |
| Learning window | Start and end shown in the plan; sessions available within it | ___ |
| Final review | Enabled or disabled; dates shown | ___ |
| Today's exam goal | Copy the displayed number and label | ___ |
| Remaining practices | Copy the progress detail's value | ___ |
| Available time | Minutes available for cards; reserve separate exam practice time | ___ |
| Reality check | Session duration and answer attempts completed | ___ |

The exam progress detail reports remaining **practices**, and prior practice contributes to progress. Future daily due counts are estimates because your answers affect scheduling. Deselected study days have lower priority, but forgetting risk or a backlog can still put practice on those days. See the [official setup and progress guidance](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9101991-preparing-for-an-exam).

Record unseen cards and due reviews separately. Don't add those counts to a remaining-practices total: that total may already include the attempts you're counting. If a number's unit is unclear, copy its label and read its explanation before using it in a calculation.

### Check the plan against time

Suppose a plan showed **420 remaining practice events** and you had **14 planned sessions** before the exam. That averages 30 events per session. If a representative session averaged 45 seconds per answer attempt, including reading corrections, those 30 attempts would take about **22.5 minutes**.

This is a capacity check using hypothetical numbers. RemNote doesn't promise equal daily queues. New concepts may take longer than familiar reviews, and incorrect answers can add work. Use your own remaining-practices figure and session timings, then allow for uneven days.

Check first introductions separately. If all 14 sessions fall within the learning window, 80 unseen cards require roughly six first introductions per session. If only ten sessions fall there, use ten as the denominator. Introducing each card once leaves its repetitions and later reviews still to do.

## Decide what each study period is for

During RemNote's learning period, a new card is repeated until two successful recalls. The optional setting for extra practice on shaky cards has a stricter rule: after a wrong answer, it asks for two successful recalls **in a row**. An optional final review schedules one last practice of every card before the exam. A catch up period temporarily raises the daily goal when you're behind. These are the separate jobs described in [RemNote's scheduler explanation](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9102040-understanding-the-exam-scheduler).

For the biology plan, check that unseen enzyme questions enter learning early enough for further practice. If you enable final review, look at the workload on its dates as well as the overall average. A manageable average can hide a busy final week. Neither a final review nor a catch up period guarantees recall on exam day.

If the projected goal exceeds your available time, first check for accidental scope: Genetics shouldn't be there. Remove duplicate questions and split prompts that require several unrelated answers; the [multi-line card guide](/blog/remnote-multi-line-cards/) can help. Then compare the revised plan with your time again. If required material still doesn't fit, decide where additional study time can come from or which course objectives need attention first. Keep the full required scope in view so you know what remains.

Accept a catch up proposal when its extra work fits sessions you can actually do. Record the temporary target and end date. If it doesn't fit, use the plan's Edit control to revisit the schedule and compare the new projection with your calendar. The [study plan guide](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/16081995-exam-study-plan) shows where that control sits. Moving the target around won't supply the missing study time.

## Audit the lecture that arrives late

Halfway through, suppose you've practiced 52 of the original 80 unseen cards at least once. That leaves 28 unseen; it doesn't tell you how many of the 52 are learned. The final lecture adds 40 more questions. You now have **68 awaiting first practice** and **280 unique cards in scope**, assuming nothing else changed.

When you add content inside an exam, RemNote displays a message saying it isn't yet in the schedule. Add the cards to the schedule from that message, or choose **Other Options** to adjust when to learn them. The weekly view breaks learning and reviews down by document, while the graph shows the plan's phases. Follow the [study plan instructions](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/16081995-exam-study-plan) for this step; creating the cards alone isn't the whole audit.

After handling the message, make three checks:

1. Find the final lecture among the exam's documents and in its weekly breakdown. Look for a recognizable question, such as an enzyme inhibition prompt, to check that the intended content is there.
2. Recheck the boundary: the 40 new cards belong; the 60 Genetics cards still don't. If the expected question is absent, follow the [missing-card investigation](/blog/remnote-cards-not-showing/) before resetting anything.
3. Update the ledger with the revised goal, remaining practices, and learning dates. With seven learning sessions left, 68 unseen cards mean about ten first introductions per session. Their repeated practice and existing reviews add to that work, so check the revised practice estimate against time again.

For daily practice, scheduled new material appears in **Learn New Content** and reviews appear in **Due Reviews**. Flashcard Home also shows warnings and proposed adjustments when an exam falls behind. The [Flashcard Home guide](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/7925835-the-flashcard-home) describes those entry points.

## Finish with a course question

After a scheduled session, close the notes and answer an exam-style question: *A competitive inhibitor is added to an enzyme reaction. Sketch how reaction rate changes with substrate concentration, and explain your prediction.* Check your response against the course's expected answer and marking criteria.

If you recall the definitions but can't build the explanation, reserve time to practice that task and improve the cards that exposed the gap. A completed daily goal records flashcard work. Your exam may also ask you to draw, reason, calculate, or connect ideas; the [flashcards and practice tests comparison](/blog/flashcards-vs-practice-tests/) helps you plan time for both.
