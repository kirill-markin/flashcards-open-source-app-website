Yesterday he wrote the rehearsal time on a scrap of paper. Every week he writes it on the board. Both sentences are about the same person doing the same thing. The first takes **كتب** (*katab*), “he wrote.” The second takes **بيكتب** (*byiktub*), “he writes.”

This worksheet uses one urban Lebanese variety of spoken Levantine Arabic. You may hear different vowels or forms from other speakers, even in Lebanon. Try to say the bold verb in each English scene. The answer key gives the verb in Arabic script and Latin letters.

## From yesterday to every week

“He **opened** the rehearsal room early yesterday” gives you **فتح** (*fataḥ*). “He **opens** the rehearsal room early every week” gives you **بيفتح** (*byiftaḥ*). In these scenes, the simple past reports a completed event and the indicative present describes a routine.

Listen to what changes between *fataḥ* and *byiftaḥ*. The present form begins with *by-*, and the first vowel of *fataḥ* drops out. With *katab* and *byiktub*, the last vowel changes too. Learn each verb as a pair so you can recall the present stem when the time cue changes. [Lebanese Arabic Institute, Book 2 lesson outline](https://www.lebanesearabicinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Lebanese-Arabic-from-Scratch-Table-of-Contents-Book-02.pdf)

## Keep the time cue and change the person

Start with *byiktub*, “he writes.” If she takes over the same weekly job, it’s **بتكتب** (*btiktub*). If we do it, it’s **منكتب** (*mniktub*). Keep *-iktub* and change the start of the word. Now move “we” to yesterday. You need **كتبنا** (*katabna*), with *-na* at the end. [Yallah Speak’s Beirut conjugation table, which writes the short vowels differently](https://www.yallahspeak.com/worksheets/lebanese-arabic/lebanese-arabic-verbs)

“Come” needs its own pair. **جيت** (*jīt*) is “I came,” and **بجي** (*biji*) is “I come.” For “he came,” use **إجا** (*ija*). Here *j* sounds like the *s* in “measure,” *sh* as in “ship,” and *ḥ* is the deeper throat sound written ح. A bar over a vowel, as in *jīt*, marks a long vowel. [Lingualism’s pronunciation guide and Table 1](https://files.lingualism.com/samples/SAMPLE-Levantine-Arabic-Verbs-Lingualism.pdf)

## Ten scenes to try aloud

Before you check the answers, say or write just the bold verb. If you get stuck, find the subject and the time cue first. Leave a blank if you’re still unsure and fill it in when you check.

1. He **writes** the number of borrowed stands on the board after every rehearsal.
2. She **drank** her coffee on the bus this morning.
3. I **came** straight here with my violin case after work yesterday.
4. He **opens** the high windows before every rehearsal.
5. We **wrote** the names of the new musicians on the back of the program last night.
6. He **ate** his lunch on the steps yesterday.
7. She **writes** a note on the score whenever we change the ending.
8. I **come** here every Tuesday to rehearse.
9. We **write** down the pieces we want to try at the start of each rehearsal.
10. They **came** in just as we were packing up last night.

## Answers and what to listen for

1. **بيكتب** (*byiktub*). “After every rehearsal” makes it a routine. For “he writes,” listen for *by-* and the *u* in the last syllable.
2. **شربت** (*shirbit*). She finished her coffee earlier. Compare *shirib*, “he drank,” with *shirbit*, “she drank.” The vowel after *r* drops out and the form ends in *-it*.
3. **جيت** (*jīt*). “I” came once, yesterday. Keep the long *ī*.
4. **بيفتح** (*byiftaḥ*). He does it before every rehearsal. Say *byiftaḥ* next to *fataḥ*, “he opened,” to hear the change.
5. **كتبنا** (*katabna*). “We” and “last night” give you the past form ending in *-na*.
6. **أكل** (*akal*). He finished his lunch yesterday. Compare *byēkul*, “he eats.” The vowel change inside the present form gives you another pair to learn.
7. **بتكتب** (*btiktub*). “Whenever” tells you it’s a habit. Use the same *-iktub* as in scene 1 and start with *bt-* for “she.”
8. **بجي** (*biji*). “Every Tuesday” makes it a routine. Put it alongside scene 3 and say *jīt* for yesterday, then *biji* for every Tuesday.
9. **منكتب** (*mniktub*). “Each rehearsal” keeps it in the present. Compare *mn-* here with *-na* in scene 5. Both verbs have “we” as the subject.
10. **إجوا** (*iju*). A group came once last night. Compare the final *u* in *iju* with the *a* in *ija*, “he came.”

## Try the two forms you mixed up tomorrow

Pick two answers that gave you trouble and read a few other scenes before you try them again. Space out the next attempts over several days. Having to produce the verb after a gap helps it stick. If you turn a scene into a flashcard, keep the subject and the time cue on the front and put the Arabic form and its pronunciation on the back.
