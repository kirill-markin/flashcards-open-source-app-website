*Das Buch* can come to mind easily while its plural, *die Bücher*, still takes a guess. Those are two separate things to retrieve. Give each its own question, and a mistake tells you which form needs another look.

The routine below is for learners who know a noun's meaning but hesitate over its article or plural. It includes a form table and twelve mixed prompts you can do on paper. Russian and English meanings identify the intended sense. The optional companion deck has **Russian prompts and explanations**, with German answers.

## Keep the noun, article, and plural together in your notes

Store a new countable noun as a small entry: **das Buch — die Bücher — book**. Then practise its singular article and plural independently. A complete reference entry and a focused recall question serve different purposes.

The forms below are **nominative**, the case used for the reference forms in this exercise. In the singular, *der* marks masculine, *die* feminine, and *das* neuter. The definite article in the nominative plural is *die* for all three genders; plural *die* doesn't make a noun feminine. The [University of Texas article reference](https://www.coerll.utexas.edu/gg/gr/det_01.html) shows these singular and plural articles. Other cases require separate practice, so learning *der Tisch* doesn't mean every sentence containing *Tisch* will use *der*; see the university's [case overview](https://www.coerll.utexas.edu/gg/gr/cas_01.html).

| Meaning: Russian / English | Singular | Plural | What changes? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| книга / book | das Buch | die Bücher | Umlaut and **-er** |
| город / city | die Stadt | die Städte | Umlaut and **-e** |
| окно / window | das Fenster | die Fenster | Noun stays the same |
| яблоко / apple | der Apfel | die Äpfel | Umlaut, no added ending |
| квартира / apartment | die Wohnung | die Wohnungen | Add **-en** |
| учительница / female teacher | die Lehrerin | die Lehrerinnen | **-in → -innen** |
| кровать / bed | das Bett | die Betten | Add **-en** |
| автомобиль / car | das Auto | die Autos | Add **-s** |

Plural formation can include an **umlaut**: a vowel change such as *a → ä*, *o → ö*, or *u → ü*. It belongs in the answer just as much as the ending. The dictionary entries for [Buch](https://www.dwds.de/wb/Buch), [Stadt](https://www.dwds.de/wb/Stadt), and [Apfel](https://www.dwds.de/wb/Apfel) give the full forms.

Use a translation to fix the meaning, then learn the German article with the German noun. For a Russian-speaking learner, *книга → das Buch* and *город → die Stadt* are useful reminders to check the German gender rather than assume it matches the Russian noun's gender.

## Ask two questions, and score only the one asked

Here is the same noun turned into two prompts. If you make your own der, die, das flashcards, give the article question its own card. The English wording below demonstrates the method; the companion deck uses Russian wording.

| Task | Front | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular article | Article in the nominative singular: **Buch — book** | **das Buch** |
| Plural form | Nominative plural: **Buch — book** | **die Bücher** |

Keep the singular article off the plural prompt so that it doesn't reveal the answer to the article question. Put the answer and a short example on the back: *Das Buch ist hier.* — “The book is here.” Read the example after answering, and leave several other nouns between the two questions about *Buch*.

Both prompts show the German noun. They test its article or plural, not whether you can translate “book” into German. To practise that later, make a separate meaning-to-German prompt with its own answer criteria.

Suppose you produce *das Buch* correctly but write *die Bucher* for the plural. Mark the article correct and the plural incorrect. Your next correction is specific: **Bücher needs the umlaut**. A single “article + plural” score would hide that distinction.

For a plural question, saying only *die* doesn't count: it is already specified for every answer in this exercise. Score the full noun, including any vowel change and ending. For an article question, say the article with the noun and score the article. Don't deduct a point for a plural you weren't asked to produce.

## Use patterns as clues, then check the whole form

A few patterns help with German noun gender and plurals. The [Leibniz Institute for the German Language's gender reference](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr@mm/6913) distinguishes word-building suffixes from less reliable tendencies based on a word's final letters.

- Nouns formed with the **-ung suffix** are feminine: *die Wohnung*, *die Rechnung*. Both have plurals in **-ungen**. The clue is the suffix, not any word that happens to end in those three letters.
- The feminine **-in** suffix gives *die Lehrerin* and *die Freundin*. Their plurals are *Lehrerinnen* and *Freundinnen*: keep the double **n**.
- Feminine nouns ending in **-e** often add **-n**: *die Lampe → die Lampen*, *die Tasche → die Taschen*. This is a useful pattern for those words, not a rule that every feminine noun follows.

Then compare the exceptions to an easy guess. [IDS's plural guide](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/progr@mm/6914) describes several plural types and their limits. *Die Hand → die Hände* and *die Stadt → die Städte* show why “feminine means add -n” fails. *Das Bett → die Betten*, *das Buch → die Bücher*, and *das Auto → die Autos* show that neuter gender alone doesn't choose an ending.

An unchanged ending also needs care. Compare *der Lehrer → die Lehrer* with *der Vater → die Väter*. Neither adds an ending, but *Vater* changes its vowel. Write the full plural in your notes instead of just “no ending.”

Meaning can matter too. This practice uses **Wörter** for separate, countable vocabulary words. *Worte* is also a real plural, used for connected or meaningful utterances; usage isn't a perfectly rigid split. [IDS explains the distinction and its limits](https://grammis.ids-mannheim.de/fragen/16). A prompt saying “individual vocabulary word” makes the intended answer clear.

## Run a short round without looking at the answers

Choose a manageable group of nouns whose meanings you already understand. Six nouns give you twelve questions when you make an article prompt and a plural prompt for each. That's a starting size, not a daily quota.

1. Read each reference entry and its example. Check an unfamiliar form in a dictionary before practising it.
2. Cover the answers and mix the two question types. Keep each noun's pair apart, then say or write the complete requested form.
3. Check after each attempt. Record the noun and the error: **article**, **ending**, or **umlaut**. A plural can have more than one error.
4. Read the corrected form, then return to that prompt after other nouns. Avoid treating an immediate echo of the answer as a fresh recall success.
5. Try the questions again in a later session, including forms you got right. Allow more time between successful reviews; bring difficult forms back sooner.

Repeated retrieval can support later memory: [Karpicke and Roediger's vocabulary experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/) found a delayed-recall benefit from continued testing after an initial correct answer. Reviewing across separate sessions also matters; [Cepeda and colleagues' spacing study](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x/) found that useful gaps depended on how long information had to be retained. These findings support recall and spaced review, without proving this particular routine or deck will produce a given result.

## Twelve mixed prompts

Cover the form table and the answer key. For **article**, write the nominative singular article plus noun. For **plural**, write *die* plus the complete nominative plural. The prompts deliberately switch tasks; they use twelve different nouns, so one answer won't reveal a later answer for the same noun.

1. **Article:** Buch — книга / book.
2. **Plural:** Stuhl — стул / chair.
3. **Article:** Stadt — город / city.
4. **Plural:** Fenster — окно / window.
5. **Article:** Apfel — яблоко / apple.
6. **Plural:** Lehrerin — учительница / female teacher.
7. **Article:** Wohnung — квартира / apartment.
8. **Plural:** Kind — ребёнок / child.
9. **Article:** Bett — кровать / bed.
10. **Plural:** Wort — отдельное слово / individual vocabulary word.
11. **Article:** Glas — стакан / drinking glass.
12. **Plural:** Zug — поезд / train.

### Answers and useful corrections

1. **das Buch.** The article question doesn't ask for *Bücher*.
2. **die Stühle.** Keep both **ü** and final **-e**.
3. **die Stadt.** This is feminine singular; *die* here doesn't mean plural.
4. **die Fenster.** The noun has no added plural ending or vowel change.
5. **der Apfel.** The singular has **A**; **Ä** belongs to its plural *Äpfel*.
6. **die Lehrerinnen.** The feminine **-in** becomes **-innen**.
7. **die Wohnung.** The **-ung** suffix is a useful gender clue here.
8. **die Kinder.** Add **-er**; there is no umlaut to add to **i**.
9. **das Bett.** Keep this article answer separate from its plural *Betten*.
10. **die Wörter.** The prompt specifies individual vocabulary words.
11. **das Glas.** The meaning here is a drinking vessel.
12. **die Züge.** Keep **ü** and final **-e**.

Count article and plural results separately, each out of six. If all six articles were right but three plurals were wrong, put those three plural prompts into the next round. The errors tell you what to practise more precisely than a combined total does.
